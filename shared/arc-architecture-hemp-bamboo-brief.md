## The materials — what they actually are

### Hemp

Hemp (Cannabis sativa) is a fast-growing annual crop. The woody core of the hemp stalk — called the hurd or shiv — is the part that matters for building. It is harvested, retted, and mixed with a lime-based binder and water to produce hempcrete. The fibre (the outer stalk) goes to textiles and paper. The hurd goes to the wall. The plant grows in a season. The wall it becomes lasts centuries.

### Hempcrete

Hempcrete is a composite building material made from the woody core of the hemp plant (hurd or shiv) bound with a lime-based binder. It is not a structural material on its own. It is laid as an infill around a load-bearing frame — typically timber — and once cured, it provides insulation, hygrothermal regulation (it breathes water vapour rather than trapping it), fire resistance, and a wall that quietly continues to absorb carbon over its working life. The material is older than concrete by centuries in some regional traditions and newer than concrete by a century in its modern revival; it sits in the interesting place where the deepest possible historical roots and the most current decarbonisation conversation meet.

Three properties matter for everything that follows:

**Composite, not monolith.** The timber frame holds the load; the hemp-and-lime infill holds the climate. Neither alone is the wall.

**Infill, not facade.** Hempcrete sits inside the wall — between the structural studs of the frame, breathing in both directions, doing its work in the body of the building. It is not cladding bolted on the outside for appearance.

**Ethical-aesthetic, not just technical.** Hempcrete is chosen for a bundle of reasons no one of which is sufficient: it works thermally, it sequesters carbon, it is locally grown, it is non-toxic, it ages honestly, it is beautiful in a way that pre-cast concrete is not. The choice is technical and ethical and aesthetic at the same time.

**In northern Ontario, hempcrete is a real conversation, not a hypothetical.** When the room already has live opinions on the material, the teaching recruits engagement that already exists rather than generating it from scratch.

### Bamboo

Bamboo is a fast-growing grass that is harvested on a cycle — the grove regrows whether you cut it or not. A bamboo field stands quietly most of the time. On harvest day, you cut. The cut becomes something else. The field remains.

In this system, bamboo names the upstream. Specifically: the XRP holding above the network reserve floor. The bamboo field stands. Rainfall flows. The reservoir holds a worked-out amount. Three different states, three different words — the same one-word-one-referent rule applied one step upstream.

Six growth stages of a planted grove serve as the visual register for savings accumulating above the network reserve in the xBuckets XRP-spring artwork. A vernacular crop framing, not a financial instrument framing. A kitchen garden framing, not an asset class framing.

## Architecture as source — not analogy

This is the most important distinction in how these materials are used.

Architecture is not being borrowed as a convenient illustration. It is the source discipline. The principle that codetry applies to the world of words is the same principle that holds when the material is wood, concrete, or hempcrete: some parts carry the load, others do not, and the parts that carry the load cannot be quietly renamed without the structure shifting underneath.

The audience already knows this. They have spent their lives in buildings. The teaching move is not to introduce a new idea. It is to make a familiar idea visible in a place they had not yet looked: the words.

When the practitioner walks into a room that came to talk about a store, a clinic, a road, or a co-op — not a software discipline — architecture is the entry point. Not metaphor. Source.

## The saltbox

A saltbox house is a vernacular cold-climate form. Asymmetric roofline: one side of the roof drops long and low to the north, forming a rear lean-to addition and shedding snow and wind. The thick north wall was not a design choice. It was the only way the house survived a boreal winter. The form is the function. Nobody built a saltbox for prettiness.

**The saltbox principle:** A codetry app is named the way a saltbox house is built — every beam carries weight. The name is the design spec. When a thing is named *Saltbox Zone 0*, that name carries: that the household is Zone 0, that this is the saltbox-house itself, and that everything else in the system flows from this centre. Change the name and you have changed the structure.

The codetry move, borrowed directly: let the form-language do the structural work. Let the name carry weight a column would otherwise carry.

**The saltbox is not a metaphor about aesthetics. It is a metaphor about who controls the load path.**

**Saltbox Zone 0 as a lived example:** Surfaces day-to-day as a homeschool companion, but the name is the design spec. It carries climate-response, household-defaultness, and vernacular-respect in one word. No scoring — a measure of a fish's worth cannot be how well it can climb a tree. The children grow brave in their own time. The dams that don't need breaking down the line are the fruit of that patience.

**The conch as counter-example:** The conch in Lord of the Flies is the cleanest worked example of a load-bearing noun going wrong. The shell is a physical object; it is also a rule; it is also a posture. Whoever holds it speaks. When the conch shatters, the discipline is not gone slowly — it is gone in the same instant. That is the saltbox principle played out in the worst case: a method whose load is held by a single fragile noun.

## Load-bearing vs decorative — the core distinction

This is the distinction that the entire architectural vocabulary runs on.

A load-bearing word is one where a rename costs something structural. If you try to rename *Jar Kitchen* to *Community Hub* and the room flinches — a small physical recoil, a sharp *no, that's not what we are* — the word was load-bearing. The rename would have taken the structure with it. No further explanation is required. The principle has not been taught at the room; it has been recovered from the room and named in their hearing.

A decorative word is one where a rename lands and nothing reacts. The word was decoration. The practitioner has just done useful diagnostic work in public. Both outcomes are codetry.

**The rename test:** Try to rename it. If the rename costs nothing — if the thing it named still stands — the word was decoration. If the rename takes something structural with it, the word was load-bearing, and it was load-bearing for a reason.

**How to run it in a room:**
1. Point at the room. *What is holding this roof up? What happens if you take that wall out?* Everyone knows. Nobody needs the vocabulary taught.
2. Cross the threshold once. *In the things you and I build out of words — a store name, a grant proposal, a co-op's bylaws, a clinic's intake form — there are also load-bearing parts. They look the same as the decorative parts on the page. They are not the same.* This is the only sentence that does the abstract work. Return immediately to the concrete.
3. Do the rename test on a word the room cares about. Not *saltbox*. Theirs. Watch what cracks.

**The flinch is the proof. The named decision is the receipt.**

Don't teach codetry as theory. Show the audience the principle they have already been using in buildings, then let them feel it on a word they care about.

## The full building materials vocabulary

This is the named lexicon. Every term is load-bearing. Remove one and something in the system loses its shape.

**Hempcrete** — Codetry itself. The binder. Lightweight, breathable, carbon-negative. Keeps strengthening after it sets. Holds the wall without being the wall. Without a frame to press against, it has nothing to do.

**The frame** — the structural skeleton the hempcrete fills around. The existing relationships, commitments, and structures you're building inside or alongside. Without a frame, there is no wall — just a pile of good material.

**The foundation** — Zone 0: ethos, honest capacity, trust map. Not a document. An accumulated practice. You cannot retroactively pour the foundation. It is either there or it is not, and no amount of planning language fills the gap.

**Windows** — intentional openings. Sized and placed deliberately. They face outward from surplus, never inward toward interior labour. You don't cut windows until the wall has cured.

**Fence post gate** — the regulatory hinge. Where inside meets outside on your terms. Set in hempcrete. Breathes. Does not seal hermetically.

**The giraffe** — something that shouldn't fit but somehow made it through. A marker of a gate that was too permissive or a wall that wasn't plumb. Named so it can be addressed.

**Drift** — what happens when a community's load-bearing noun is translated — even into a cleaner, more general, or more reusable noun — without the community's recognition or consent. Drift is not announced as a loss. Each move feels like cleanup, like progress, like professionalism. The codetry-test exists because drift is invisible to the type checker and obvious to the person who handed you the word.

## Hempcrete — every distinct use in this system

### 1. Codetry IS hempcrete (the discipline)

The one-sentence claim — stable, unchanged since it was written:

> Codetry is hempcrete: a composite discipline whose names are the breathing infill laid between the structural frame of type and the surface skin of affordance, doing the climate-regulating, carbon-sequestering, ethical-aesthetic work that no single layer in the system was holding before.

The discipline itself is the infill. Not the frame (the existing system). Not the facade (signage, documentation bolted on the outside). The naming practice is what goes between structural type and surface affordance and does its climate-regulating work from inside the wall.

Strip the infill and the frame still stands — but the wall stops regulating, the room stops being habitable in the same way, and the mechanics that were quietly held by the composite now have to be shouted by signage and policy.

Three reasons the metaphor fits:
- Composite of old materials. Codetry is Ubiquitous Language + System Metaphor + Nielsen #2 + Lakoff + content design + naming-things — old ingredients, new mix.
- Infill, not frame. Codetry is non-load-bearing; a codetry-disciplined product still needs the structural frame of working code, real flows, a coherent domain model. Codetry alone cannot hold a product up. Codetry inside a sound frame makes the product breathable and warm.
- Ethical-aesthetic as well as functional. The metaphor IS the architecture and the product carries the dignity of meaning what it says. The aesthetic is part of the spec, not a finish applied at the end.

### 2. The hempcrete toolbox (the practitioner's method)

The 4-step practitioner creative and handover method:

1. Pick the word — chosen strategically to bear weight for the year (not what you want; what the moment needs)
2. Mold hempcrete around it — organic + lime, breathable, gets stronger as it cures, can be held while forming
3. Hold it until it can be held by someone else — the object becomes physical and real
4. Hand it off — not as a gift but as theirs. The community keeps it. That's the art. That's the exit.

Hempcrete vs concrete: Concrete seals. Hempcrete insulates without trapping. The thing inside doesn't suffocate. It cures stronger than the word alone.

A document built this way is a hempcrete object. It gets stronger as agents add to it. It breathes. It does not trap what's inside.

### 3. Gates are hempcrete

The base formula for a zone gate: a structural material that breathes, insulates, and flexes — it does not seal hermetically. Hempcrete admits moisture exchange while resisting structural damage. A gate admits identity signals while resisting composable reverse-lookup paths. The gate that seals hermetically is not a gate — it is a wall.

Each gate gets the mix it needs for its specific crossing. The base formula stays constant; the context-specific additives vary.

### 4. The community vocabulary boundary is hempcrete

The boundary that holds the community's language is set in hempcrete. Not drywall. Not paint. It cures over time. Hardens with age and humidity. Draws carbon out of the air instead of adding to it. And like hempcrete, it requires regular inspection.

The practitioner does not set the vocabulary boundary and leave. The inspection is built in from the start. Drift is not dramatic. It is slow. It looks like a reasonable substitution. The inspection catches it before the compound interest of a wrong name runs for another quarter.

Goodwill compounds. A community whose vocabulary holds — whose Jar Kitchen is still called the Jar Kitchen, whose board can explain the 84¢ figure without referring to a consultant — that community has built something whose value increases with each generation that inherits it. The language is the asset. The regular inspection is the maintenance schedule.

### 5. The build sequence — Step 2 of 4

The On-Ramp has four steps. Hempcrete is step two.

**Step 1 — Lay the frame.** The structural skeleton. What is inside, who is inside, what the walls protect. Most people skip this. They want to fill the wall before the frame is plumb. Two posts and a beam is a frame. You can add to it.

**Step 2 — Fill with hempcrete.** Hempcrete is not a structural material — the frame does that. Hempcrete is the binder. It fills, insulates, regulates. It holds the wall without being the wall. In Codetry that binder is the practice itself: the vocabulary, the naming discipline, the zone model. You do not pour all at once. You fill in lifts — a foot at a time, tamped, left to set before the next. Rushing it produces a weak wall. The pace is the point. A hempcrete wall is a nice-looking wall — no maintenance overhead of drywall, no scuffs, no repainting every few years, no paper face that dents when someone bumps it. The material argument and the framework argument are the same argument.

**Step 3 — Frame the windows.** Intentional openings. Sized and placed deliberately. You don't cut windows until the wall has cured.

**Step 4 — Hang the gate.** The regulatory hinge — where inside meets outside on your terms.

**Permission to stray:** A build sequence is a starting frame, not a contract. Real walls meet real conditions. The hempcrete mix changes with the humidity. The frame settles. You adapt.

### 6. Founding mode vs reclamation mode

The materials are the same. The order of operations is not.

**Founding mode** — hempcrete from day one. Generationally durable. The house gets stronger while you live in it. What you build this way, you can hand to someone else without explaining what it secretly is.

**Reclamation mode** — board-by-board sovereignty recovery within a house someone else built. You cannot demolish while a thousand people are living inside. Each replaced board must carry the weight of the ones around it while the old ones come out. Most people arriving are in reclamation mode. That is not a lesser version. It is harder, it carries more scar tissue, and it produces a house that is genuinely yours by the time you are done.

Handing the wrong kit to the wrong mode: a founding-mode toolkit handed to a reclamation-mode practitioner produces paralysis or destruction. A reclamation-mode toolkit handed to a founder produces timidity. Read the site before you mix.

### 7. Why hempcrete teaches better than concrete

Concrete teaches load-bearing, but it teaches only one thing at once. Hempcrete teaches the codetry-specific point: a single material can be load-bearing and insulating and breathable and community-buildable and locally legal all at the same time. One name, multiple structural jobs.

*Saltbox* carries climate-response and household-defaultness and vernacular-respect in one word. *Headwaters* carries source-of-the-water and small-and-upstream and what-flows-from-here in one word. The community member who has thought about hempcrete versus concrete already understands that picking the material is itself an architectural decision, not a budget decision. Picking the word reads as the same kind of decision, not as marketing.

### 8. Where hempcrete strains — named honestly

Hempcrete needs a frame to hold any real load. Codetry needs an existing system to hold any real load.

Hempcrete cures slowly. A renamed system takes weeks of conversation to settle into the team's mouths.

The lime binder is less ecologically clean than the hemp suggests. The rename test depends on a practitioner who can tell load-bearing from decorative, and that practitioner's judgment is itself a binder whose composition matters.

The metaphor is not flattering by accident. The hempcrete wall is the kind of wall the practitioner is trying to build because it is honest about what it can and cannot carry.

### 9. Hempcrete as visual texture

The hempcrete texture photograph is used as a background image on the Odyssey page of the Codetry Ship artifact. The visual language is consistent — hempcrete as texture, not just as concept.

## Bamboo — the full story

### What bamboo means in this system

The bamboo field is the upstream. Specifically: the XRP holding above the 10-XRP network reserve floor.

Three states, three words — the same one-word-one-referent rule applied one step upstream:
- **The bamboo field stands.** The XRP holding above the reserve. Quietly present most days. A working crop, not an asset class.
- **Rainfall flows.** The RLUSD that moves into the budget buckets.
- **The reservoir holds.** A worked-out amount. The reserve floor.

The field is not the wall of the building; it is part of what the building is downstream of.

**Why bamboo and not portfolio:** Calling XRP "the bamboo field" refuses the portfolio register. It frames the holding the way a kitchen garden frames a tomato plant: a working part of the household, not an investment position. That matches the Zone 1 voice, and it keeps the dignity-of-meaning-what-it-says rule that the hempcrete finding names.

**The harvest move:** On payday, you cut some — the swap to RLUSD — and the cut bamboo becomes rainfall that fills the reservoir. The field regrows. You walk past it most days without noticing.

Six growth stages of a planted bamboo grove serve as the visual register for savings accumulating above the network reserve in the xBuckets XRP-spring artwork. Each growth stage maps to a savings threshold. The grove is the living register.

### The bamboo-hempcrete distinction

**Hempcrete = the discipline.** The infill, the binder, what holds the naming practice together. The metaphor for what codetry is.

**Bamboo = a surface register.** The visual and conceptual language on a specific feature — the upstream, the savings artwork. The metaphor for what XRP is in this system.

The two are not interchangeable. Hempcrete is the wall. Bamboo is the field the building draws from. Different rooms, different materials, different jobs.

### Where bamboo strains — named honestly

Real bamboo establishes over years and is famously hard to remove (rhizomes). XRP swings in hours, and a holding can crash in ways a grove cannot. The metaphor is true on regeneration and vernacular; looser on cadence and reversibility. Naming the leak is itself the codetry move. We bound every metaphor we use.

### FL.3 — The bamboo field test (the first formally rejected test)

**The question:** Does the word *bamboo field* earn a place on the wallet/XRP-swap chip, or does it stay as a lineage-doc reference only?

**What was tested:** Three chip wordings on the canvas:
- Variant 0 (control): *412 XRP unconverted ⇄ Swap to ~$420 RLUSD* — mechanical, register-neutral
- Variant A (full metaphor): *Bamboo field standing ✂ Cut ~$420 into rainfall* — wallet becomes field, swap becomes cut, no XRP word visible
- Variant B (half-metaphor): *Bamboo field · 412 XRP ⇄ Swap to ~$420 RLUSD* — XRP retained as anchor noun, bamboo field added as label

**What happened:**
- Variant 0 reads as plumbing. Exactly what it should be.
- Variant A breaks. *Bamboo field standing* leaves a cold reader with no idea what the chip touches. *Cut into rainfall* asks the reader to hold biological cutting and atmospheric rainfall in the same gesture — the metaphor cracks at the verb. Two unreconciled registers (water and plant) competing for the reader's attention.
- Variant B is the most interesting failure. It doesn't crack — it just adds noise. The XRP word still does all the load-bearing work. *Bamboo field* sits as a nickname that gives the reader nothing they didn't already have.

**The rule discovered:** One register per screen. A single product surface should carry a single metaphor register, not two competing ones. A metaphor that lives honestly in the lineage doc may still be the wrong word for a surface, if that surface already belongs to a different register.

**Status:** Rejected for the wallet chip. The bamboo-field metaphor stays as a lineage-doc reference. The verdict would change only if a bamboo-only surface is built (the upstream gets its own screen, no rainfall language nearby) or if the water register is replaced wholesale on the Payday tab — neither of which is on the table.

This is where The On-Ramp came from. Every use of the build sequence, the tradesperson argument, and the founding/reclamation distinction traces back to this:

> "There are so many zone 0 humans, husbands and wives, carrying all the weight of the world on their shoulders. The people who have been holding the headwaters are tired and looking for new blood to get out of the grindstone and join the movement. We just have to make it easy to hop aboard with codetry. Teach them the trace of laying the frame, board by board, filling with hempcrete, framing the windows and on and on. > "North Star should be where these ideas go to be shaped I think. This should be shaped with my husband alongside, the men in my life from homesteader to mechanic to plumber to electrician — speaking their language along the way. They can build things — just let them get to work with a plan that they can stray from and revamp continuously with nobody interjecting while they work and women coming behind to pick up the scraps and ten to the dump and talk to the outside world so they can have their hands filled with on the ground real world things. The oil in the grindstone. That engine doesn't grind without these guys — and the future is looking bright for the oil. Let's help get the youth of tomorrow hands greased."
