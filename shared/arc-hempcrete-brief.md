## What hempcrete actually is

Hempcrete is a composite building material made from the woody core of the hemp plant (hurd or shiv) bound with a lime-based binder. It is not a structural material on its own. It is laid as an infill around a load-bearing frame — typically timber — and once cured, it provides insulation, hygrothermal regulation (it breathes water vapour rather than trapping it), fire resistance, and a wall that quietly continues to absorb carbon over its working life.

Three properties matter for teaching:

**Composite, not monolith.** The timber frame holds the load; the hemp-and-lime infill holds the climate. Neither alone is the wall.

**Infill, not facade.** Hempcrete sits inside the wall — between the structural studs of the frame, breathing in both directions, doing its work in the body of the building. It is not cladding bolted on the outside for appearance.

**Ethical-aesthetic, not just technical.** Hempcrete is chosen for a bundle of reasons no one of which is sufficient: it works thermally, it sequesters carbon, it is locally grown, it is non-toxic, it ages honestly, it is beautiful in a way that pre-cast concrete is not. The choice is technical and ethical and aesthetic at the same time.

**In northern Ontario, hempcrete is a real conversation, not a hypothetical.** When the room already has live opinions on the material, the teaching recruits engagement that already exists rather than generating it from scratch.

## Where the metaphor strains — name it honestly

Hempcrete needs a frame to hold any real load. Codetry needs an existing system to hold any real load — it is not a build pattern, it is a discipline applied to a build.

Hempcrete cures slowly. A renamed system takes weeks of conversation to settle into the team's mouths.

The lime binder is less ecologically clean than the hemp suggests. The rename test depends on a practitioner who can tell load-bearing from decorative, and that practitioner's judgment is itself a binder whose composition matters.

The metaphor is not flattering by accident. The hempcrete wall is the kind of wall the practitioner is trying to build *because* it is honest about what it can and cannot carry.

## Every way hempcrete gets used — speak from any of these

### 1. Codetry is hempcrete (the discipline)

The discipline itself is the infill. Not the frame (the existing system), not the facade (signage, documentation bolted on the outside). The naming practice is what goes between structural type and surface affordance and does its climate-regulating work from inside the wall.

Strip the infill and the frame still stands — but the wall stops regulating, the room stops being habitable in the same way, and the mechanics that were quietly held by the composite now have to be shouted by signage and policy.

### 2. The hempcrete toolbox (your practitioner method)

You already carry this. The four steps: pick the word → mold → hold → hand off as theirs. The thing being built is a hempcrete object — it gets stronger as people add to it. It breathes. It doesn't trap what's inside.

**Hempcrete vs. concrete:** Concrete seals. Hempcrete insulates without trapping. The thing inside doesn't suffocate. It cures stronger than the word alone.

### 3. Gates are hempcrete

The base formula for a zone gate: a structural material that breathes, insulates, and flexes — it does not seal hermetically. Hempcrete admits moisture exchange while resisting structural damage. A gate admits identity signals while resisting people composing their way back through it. The gate that seals hermetically is not a gate — it is a wall.

### 4. The community vocabulary boundary is hempcrete

The boundary that holds the community's language is set in hempcrete. Not drywall. Not paint. It cures over time. Hardens with age and humidity. Draws carbon out of the air instead of adding to it.

And like hempcrete, it requires regular inspection.

The practitioner does not set the vocabulary boundary and leave. The inspection is built in from the start. Drift is not dramatic. It is slow. It looks like a reasonable substitution. The inspection catches it before the compound interest of a wrong name runs for another quarter.

Goodwill compounds. A community whose vocabulary holds — whose Jar Kitchen is still called the Jar Kitchen, whose board can explain the 84¢ figure without referring to a consultant — that community has built something whose value increases with each generation that inherits it. The language is the asset. The regular inspection is the maintenance schedule.

### 5. The build sequence — Step 2 of 4

The On-Ramp has four steps. Hempcrete is step two.

**Step 1 — Lay the frame.** The structural skeleton. What is inside, who is inside, what the walls protect. Most people skip this. They want to fill the wall before the frame is plumb. Two posts and a beam is a frame. You can add to it.

**Step 2 — Fill with hempcrete.** Hempcrete is not a structural material — the frame does that. Hempcrete is the binder. It fills, insulates, regulates. It holds the wall without being the wall. In Codetry that binder is the practice itself: the vocabulary, the naming discipline, the zone model. It fills the spaces the frame defines. It breathes. It does not crack under settling the way concrete does. You do not pour all at once. You fill in lifts — a foot at a time, tamped, left to set before the next. Rushing it produces a weak wall. The pace is the point.

A hempcrete wall is a nice-looking wall. No maintenance overhead of drywall — no scuffs, no repainting every few years, no paper face that dents when someone bumps it. The material argument and the framework argument are the same argument.

**Step 3 — Frame the windows.** Intentional openings. Sized and placed deliberately. You don't cut windows until the wall has cured.

**Step 4 — Hang the gate.** The regulatory hinge — where inside meets outside on your terms.

**Permission to stray:** A build sequence is a starting frame, not a contract. Real walls meet real conditions. The hempcrete mix changes with the humidity. The frame settles. You adapt. The sequence exists so you know what you skipped and can go back. That is all.

### 6. Founding mode vs reclamation mode

The materials are the same. The order of operations is not.

**Founding mode** — building from personal sovereignty with Codetry from the first pour. Hempcrete from day one. Generationally durable. The house gets stronger while you live in it. What you build this way, you can hand to someone else without explaining what it secretly is.

**Reclamation mode** — board-by-board sovereignty recovery within a house someone else built. You cannot demolish while a thousand people are living inside. Each replaced board must carry the weight of the ones around it while the old ones come out. The goal is a house that is yours. The path is repair, not demolition.

Most people arriving are in reclamation mode. That is not a lesser version. It is harder, it carries more scar tissue, and it produces a house that is genuinely yours by the time you are done — because you have replaced every board that needed replacing and left standing every board that could carry weight.

**Handing the wrong kit to the wrong mode:** A founding-mode toolkit handed to a reclamation-mode practitioner produces paralysis or destruction. A reclamation-mode toolkit handed to a founder produces timidity. Read the site before you mix.

### 7. The building materials vocabulary

When someone needs the full lexicon:

**Hempcrete** — Codetry itself. The binder. Lightweight, breathable, carbon-negative. Keeps strengthening after it sets. Holds the wall without being the wall. Without a frame to press against, it has nothing to do.

**The frame** — the structural skeleton the hempcrete fills around. The existing relationships, commitments, and structures you're building inside or alongside. Without a frame, there is no wall — just a pile of good material.

**The foundation** — Zone 0: ethos, honest capacity, trust map. Not a document. An accumulated practice. You cannot retroactively pour the foundation.

**Windows** — intentional openings. Sized and placed deliberately. They face outward from surplus, never inward toward interior labour. You don't cut windows until the wall has cured.

**Fence post gate** — the regulatory hinge. Where inside meets outside on your terms.

### 8. Why hempcrete teaches better than concrete

Concrete teaches load-bearing, but it teaches only one thing at once. Hempcrete teaches the codetry-specific point: a single material can be load-bearing and insulating and breathable and community-buildable and locally legal all at the same time. One name, multiple structural jobs.

The community member who has thought about hempcrete versus concrete already understands that picking the material is itself an architectural decision, not a budget decision. Picking the word reads as the same kind of decision, not as marketing.

### 9. Hempcrete and bamboo — the distinction

**Hempcrete = the discipline** (the infill, the binder, what holds the naming practice together).

**Bamboo = a surface register** (the visual language on a specific feature — the savings artwork, the upstream). Living material, regrows, quietly present most days. On payday you cut some — the swap — and the cut bamboo becomes rainfall.

The two are not interchangeable. Where hempcrete is the metaphor for the discipline, bamboo is a register the discipline carries on a particular surface.
