# Resource allocation as the highest economic act — source + editor's note

**Source.** A short essay (Musk-attributed, 2025) on capital allocation, the Mises calculation problem, and the entrepreneur-vs-bureaucrat split. Copy of the source text is in `attached_assets/Pasted-Elon-Musk-had-said-something-that-really-stuck-with-me-_1777486646492.txt`.

**Status.** External reference material. Not codetry doctrine. Not a community-of-practice text. Held here so codetry's stance on allocation has a citable point of contrast.

## Why it sits in the library

The library is a research collection. This essay is one of the cleanest short statements of the founder-as-allocator school — the lineage that runs from Mises (1920) and Hayek through to the modern Silicon Valley entrepreneur frame. Codetry agrees with parts of that school and breaks with parts of it. The handbook's chapter on the disagreement (`§2.5 — Different from capital allocation at scale`) needs the original to argue against. This file is the original, with notes.

## Three things the essay gets right (codetry agrees)

1. **The calculation problem is real.** A central planner cannot see what the people on the ground see. Mises' 1920 argument still cuts. Codetry's whole reason for existing is the same observation pulled one layer down: the words a community uses carry information no head office can reconstruct.

2. **Profit is a signal, not a goal.** A profitable allocation means people valued the work enough to pay for it. A loss means the bet was wrong. Codetry agrees. The Headwaters store plan is built so the price page, the cost stack, and the truck cost are public — the signal is made legible at the household level, not just at the cap table.

3. **Skin in the game changes allocator quality.** A person allocating their own balance sheet under personal accountability allocates better than a committee with no exposure. The Northern Band ask is structurally a Musk-shaped bet: $1.08M, $90k/month, twelve months, personal accountability through Parr's Workshop Inc. If Bobbie is wrong, she's out.

## Three things the essay gets wrong (codetry departs)

1. **The entrepreneur/bureaucrat binary misses the practitioner.** The essay frames the world as founders (good) versus bureaucrats (bad). Codetry adds a third actor: the practitioner. Personal risk like the entrepreneur. Finite tenure like the bureaucrat. But neither rent-compounding nor rent-collecting — rent-transferring. Small scale, written method, deliberate handover. Most productive work in a real economy looks like this, not like SpaceX.

2. **Private monopoly breaks Mises the same way state monopoly does.** The essay's clean entrepreneur/bureaucrat split assumes private actors are calculation-respecting and state actors are not. The Northern Store in Northern Band — a private monopoly with no exposed cost stack — behaves exactly like a Gosplan: shortages, hidden rationing, no information flowing back. The 42¢ stat in the walkthrough is the giveaway. Both states and monopolies break the calculation problem; codetry's answer applies to both.

3. **Compound is not the only honest exit.** The essay's success story is the founder who allocates well and then keeps allocating, on a bigger and bigger pile, for as long as they live. Codetry inserts a different exit: handover. The practitioner is paid to write the method down so the community can run the work without them. A successful practitioner is one the community no longer needs in the chair.

## How to cite this

In codetry-aligned writing, name it as: *"the founder-as-allocator school (Mises 1920, modern entrepreneur lineage)"* — not as "Musk says." The lineage is the point; the spokesperson is incidental.
