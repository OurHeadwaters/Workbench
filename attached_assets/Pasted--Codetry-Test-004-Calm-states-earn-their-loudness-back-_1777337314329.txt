# Codetry Test 004 — Calm states earn their loudness back

**Date:** 2026-04-26
**Zone:** 1 — Headwaters / xBuckets (daily kitchen garden)
**App:** xbuckets PWA, Payday tab
**Tester:** Bobbie + Replit Agent
**Surface tested:** the live Payday tab — two precedents already shipped in
production, named here for the first time
**Files:**

- `artifacts/xbuckets/src/pages/PaydayPlanner.tsx` (Wobble targets escape
  hatch around line 1605; Tinker disclosure wiring around line 750)
- `artifacts/xbuckets/src/components/PaydayRestCard.tsx` (Tinker button
  render around line 237)

---

## The finding (one line)

Two independent design moves on the same screen — Test 001's "Wobble
targets" escape hatch and Task #806's "Tinker with the plan" disclosure —
arrived at the same shape: a calm primary surface with the loud controls
retreating behind a quiet, summonable text-link. Two unrelated authors
converging on one shape isn't a coincidence; it's a rule asking to be
named.

## What was on screen (the convergence itself)

Both surfaces were designed for different reasons by different motions of
the work, and yet the silhouette they cut on the screen is identical.

### Precedent 1 — Wobble targets (Test 001)

When Test 001 collapsed the percentage-chip cluster ("Full rain / 90% /
75% / 50% / Custom") and replaced it with a single "Channel the rainfall"
CTA, the *ability* to dial down spending targets when rainfall was short
still had to live somewhere. The old chip cluster solved this by always
being loud — five buttons sitting peer-of-CTA across the top of the
catchment plan.

The graduation chose differently. The custom-scale control retreated
behind a single quiet text-link sitting under the flow ribbon
(`PaydayPlanner.tsx` ~ line 1631):

```tsx
<button …
  className="text-[12px] font-black uppercase tracking-[0.14em]
             underline underline-offset-2 transition
             text-white/55 hover:text-white/85">
  {COPY.paydayExecute.customScaleButton}   {/* "Wobble targets" */}
</button>
```

When tapped, the wobble panel unfolds in place and the user can dial. The
default surface stays calm: one CTA, one ribbon, one short hint. The
loudness is *summoned* on demand, not pre-loud.

### Precedent 2 — Tinker with the plan (Task #806)

When Task #806 introduced the "all funded" rest view — the calm state
that holds the screen between the moment the cycle is funded and the next
payday — the same question recurred at one register higher: what happens
to the *active controls* (Rainfall card, Auto-bills, Catchment Plan)
when the cycle no longer needs them?

The old surface answered: leave them visible. Funded or not, the planner
stayed loud. The Task #806 graduation answered differently. The
PaydayRestCard takes the screen, and a single quiet disclosure button
sits at its foot (`PaydayRestCard.tsx` ~ line 237):

```tsx
<button …
  aria-expanded={tinkerOpen}
  aria-controls="payday-planner-disclosure"
  className="… text-sm font-black text-white/75
             transition hover:border-cyan-300/40 hover:text-cyan-200">
  <span>{COPY.paydayRest.tinkerLabel}</span>   {/* "Tinker with the plan" */}
  {tinkerOpen ? <ChevronDown … /> : <ChevronRight … />}
</button>
```

The wired controls aren't gone — they're conditionally rendered exactly
when the user asks for them (`PaydayPlanner.tsx` line 764):

```tsx
{(!showRestView || tinkerOpen) && (
  <>
    {/* Income card, Auto-bills, Catchment Plan */}
  </>
)}
```

Same shape: calm primary surface (the rest card), quiet text-link with a
chevron, summon-on-demand panel. Same silhouette, drawn at a different
register. Different author intent, different originating task, different
copy — and yet the geometry is the same to the pixel.

### The convergence is the data

Two independent design decisions, made months apart for unrelated reasons
on the same screen, settled on the same physical shape:

1. **calm primary surface holds the screen by default**
2. **the loud controls don't disappear — they retreat one click away**
3. **the access affordance is a quiet text-link, not a peer CTA**
4. **the summoned panel unfolds in place, doesn't navigate away**

When two unrelated motions of the work converge on the same shape, the
shape is doing something the authors haven't yet named. This codetry
test is the naming.

## The intervention

There is no code change here. The intervention is the act of recognition:
seeing that the two surfaces are instances of one rule, and writing the
rule down so the next surface that needs the same shape can reach for it
deliberately rather than rediscover it accidentally.

## The rule discovered

> **Surface state mirrors cycle state, and calm states earn their
> loudness back.** A calm state is the default surface for any
> architectural state that doesn't require user action. The loud
> controls don't disappear — they retreat behind a quiet, named
> disclosure that the user can summon on demand. The default is calm;
> loudness has to earn itself back by being asked for.

This is a different rule family from one-word-one-referent (Test 001) and
one-register-per-screen (Test 003). Those rules govern the *vocabulary*
of the surface — which words appear, in which dialect. This rule governs
the *temperature* of the surface — which controls appear, at what
loudness, given the architectural state behind the screen.

The two families compose:

- **Vocabulary rules** answer *which words?* on a screen.
- **Temperature rules** answer *which controls, how loud?* on a screen.

A surface can satisfy one family and break the other. A locked
vocabulary doesn't help if every control is shouting at peer volume; a
calm layout doesn't help if the words are doing two jobs each. Both
families have to hold for the surface to feel right.

## Falsifiable claim

This rule is falsified if either of the following holds on a Zone 1
surface:

1. A surface is in a calm architectural state (no user action required —
   cycle funded, plan complete, nothing pending) and still presents loud
   active controls by default. *Failure mode A: pre-loud.*
2. A surface hides a loud control so successfully that a power user who
   knows the control exists cannot find it within one tap from the calm
   default. *Failure mode B: hidden, not retreated.*

The diagnostic move when a calm screen feels wrong: ask whether it is
pre-loud (controls in your face when they don't need to be) or
*so*-calm-it's-hostile (controls actually unreachable). The rule sits
between those two failure modes. Retreat, not removal.

## Status

**Graduated on arrival.** Unlike Tests 001–003, this rule was not
discovered on the canvas and then graduated to production — it was
*shipped twice* in production before being named, by two unrelated
tasks, and only then recognised as a single rule. The codetry test here
is the naming, not the shipping.

- Precedent 1 — Wobble targets escape hatch — shipped in the Test 001
  graduation (`PaydayPlanner.tsx`, RainfallFlowRibbon block).
- Precedent 2 — Tinker with the plan disclosure — shipped in Task #806
  (PaydayRestCard, the calm "all funded" rest view).
- This doc — the recognition + naming — adds no production code or copy.

## See also

- `docs/codetry/001-payday-words-bearing-weight.md` — the test that
  produced the Wobble targets escape hatch as a side-effect of demoting
  the percentage-chip cluster. Vocabulary rule (one word → one
  referent). The temperature rule named here is *complementary*, not a
  replacement: Test 001 governs which words appear; Test 004 governs
  which controls appear at what loudness.
- `docs/codetry/003-bamboo-field-on-wallet-chip.md` — the
  one-register-per-screen rule. Same complementarity: 003 governs the
  metaphor register of the surface; 004 governs the temperature of the
  controls on it.
- `docs/codetry/002-zone-1-words-locked.md` — the locked Zone 1 word
  map. The temperature rule does not change which words a surface uses,
  but it does decide whether those words appear pre-loud or summoned.

## What this rule does not cover

- The locked-vocabulary machine guard (forbidden-strings test, lint
  rule, copy-snapshot diff) raised by Test 001a's Sighting 2. That is a
  vocabulary-rule enforcement question. Separate concern, separate task.
- An audit of the rest of the app for places this rule is *not yet*
  held. Candidate follow-up (a Test 004a or 004-audit), but explicitly
  out of scope here. This task is the naming; the audit is its own
  motion.
- A formal taxonomy of "calm" vs "loud" — those are working words for
  now, anchored by the two precedents. If a future surface needs a
  third or fourth temperature it will be on that test to coin and
  defend the additional word.
