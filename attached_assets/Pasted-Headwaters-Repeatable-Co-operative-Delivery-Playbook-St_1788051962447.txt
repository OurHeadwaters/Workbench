Headwaters Repeatable Co-operative Delivery Playbook
Status: Canonical internal delivery method
Owner: Headwaters practitioner / platform owner
Version date: 30 August 2026
Applies to: A new white-label co-operative or community organization receiving a bounded Headwaters implementation

This is the operating source of truth for scoping and delivering a co-op engagement. The 807 reference record is in 807-reference-implementation.md, the fillable working forms are in templates/headwaters-coop-delivery-templates.md, and the paper rehearsal is in headwaters-second-coop-dry-run.md.

1. The method in one sentence
Map the co-operative once, launch one useful operating layer, teach the people who run it, report what changed, and leave a ranked path for the next tool.

The first engagement is a bounded capacity-building project. It is not a requirement to buy every Headwaters product, a transfer of governance to Headwaters, or a promise that every planned connection is already available.

What is included in the initial implementation
Codetry organizational map: the co-op's purpose, work, decision rights, relationships, information boundaries, vocabulary, and funder-facing language.
Codetry Vocabulary Ledger and governance setup: a versioned language layer with an approval trail. It does not replace bylaws, financial policies, board resolutions, or legal advice.
Field Guide Finance: a co-op-specific edition built from reusable finance modules and the co-op's approved language. It covers practical finance rhythms, records, budgets, cash flow, tax/compliance decision points, and funding readiness. It is educational guidance, not accounting or legal advice.
One selected operational tool: chosen from the readiness matrix after discovery. The first tool must solve a named problem for a named operator group.
Configuration and intake: approved tenant identity, regional settings, roles, content, operating rhythms, and a bounded source-data handoff.
Training: role-based sessions plus reference materials and a named tenant operator.
Launch and initial results: an acceptance check, support path, baseline, and a first report of observable changes. A report may say “not measured yet”; it must not invent impact.
Later-tool roadmap: a prioritized, fundable sequence showing what the existing map enables and what still requires configuration, integration, migration, approval, or new product work.
Helping Hands, mobile access, or another companion module may be included where discovery shows a clear fit. It is not silently guaranteed as part of every first implementation.

Payer and funding language
The payer is named in the scope and budget:

Direct purchase: the co-operative pays Headwaters under the signed scope.
Co-op grant: the co-operative receives or uses approved grant funding and pays Headwaters for the implementation.
Sponsor-supported work: a sponsor pays for a defined common resource or an agreed implementation. The sponsor does not acquire the co-op's governance, member data, or tenant administration.
Field Guide Finance can be provided at no cost to a participating co-op under a common sponsor arrangement while the implementation remains a funded $20,000 or $28,000 project. Never call the implementation “free” because the Field Guide is free to the participant. State who funds the work, what Headwaters is paid to do, and what the co-op receives.

Alterna wording gate
Alterna is not a confirmed sponsor or partner unless written approval is recorded for the exact use.

Safe before approval: “Designed for co-operative funding conversations, with the 807 deployment serving as a reference case for proposed Alterna-supported work.”
Safe when describing a proposal: “Proposed with support from Alterna Savings,” only when that proposal is actually submitted or discussed in that context and does not imply approval.
Do not use “Brought to you by Alterna Savings,” an Alterna logo, an endorsement, or a confirmed sponsorship claim until Alterna has expressly approved the wording and brand use in writing.
The approval record belongs in the sponsor decision register, not in marketing copy by assumption.

3. Boundaries and working principles
Headwaters, the tenant, and the sponsor
Party	Controls	Does not control
Headwaters	Platform code, shared infrastructure, release process, implementation method, tenant provisioning, technical security controls, scoped configuration, training materials, and support agreement	The co-op's board decisions, member policy, accounting sign-off, legal interpretation, sponsor approval, or another tenant's data
Co-op board / authorized tenant sponsor	Purpose, priorities, tenant identity, vocabulary approval, membership and operating policy, who gets tenant-level roles, launch acceptance, data sharing, and roadmap order	Headwaters platform administration, another tenant, shared infrastructure credentials, or unapproved product promises
Tenant operator(s)	Day-to-day content, schedules, member communications, routine checks, and first-line issue reporting within the granted role	Platform-wide configuration, other tenants, production secrets, or changes to legal/compliance policy
Funding sponsor	The approved funding purpose, eligible budget framing, sponsor participation, and any approved attribution or brand use	The co-op's governance, private records, platform administration, or the right to approve work outside the funding agreement
Review boundaries
Escalate before committing when work touches:

Legal: bylaws, member rights, contracts, privacy promises, data transfer, liability, intellectual property, or an unapproved sponsor relationship.
Accounting / tax: tax treatment, HST/GST, payroll, remittances, financial statements, restricted funds, or a control that changes the treasurer's responsibilities.
Security / privacy: new credentials, identity-provider changes, personal or financial data migration, public report links, retention, or cross-tenant access.
Integration: an external system, payment provider, mailbox, calendar, domain, API, or data import that is not already in the approved standard configuration.
Custom product work: a new feature, non-standard workflow, or requirement that cannot be met by configuration.

Change control
Anything that changes the selected tool, data classes, external system, legal/compliance interpretation, delivery date, or price is a scope change. The owner records:

requested change and reason;
affected decision, data, people, and acceptance check;
price/timing/security impact;
approver;
accepted, deferred, declined, or separately quoted status.
6. Configuration discipline
The configuration decision register is the protection against turning 807's choices into platform defaults.

Classification rules
Platform default: safe reusable behavior or wording maintained by Headwaters for every tenant. It must not contain 807 names, prices, region assumptions, or private policy.
Regional default: a starting point for a geography or climate. It is proposed, not silently applied. The tenant accepts, edits, or rejects it.
Tenant decision: the co-op's own name, roles, tiers, vocabulary, schedules, policies, sources, content, or integrations.
Sponsor decision: funding purpose, reporting format, common Field Guide language, participation, attribution, and brand wording.
Custom work: anything requiring new code, a non-standard data migration, a new integration, a legal/accounting/security review, or a separate quote.
Minimum register fields
decision_id · topic · proposed_value · classification · source/reason · tenant_impact · data_sensitivity · approver · approval_date · status · implementation_reference · review_date

The operator sees tenant-level settings and content. They do not receive access to Headwaters platform administration, shared tenant registry controls, other tenants, production secrets, or deployment credentials.

7. Operations, recovery, and handoff
Required before launch
Tenant access: named board sponsor, tenant operator, backup operator, and role matrix; leavers have a removal path.
Domain and auth: verified domain decision, sign-in test, recovery contact, and a clear note if custom-domain work is not included.
Payments: the tenant's payment owner, account, settlement expectations, webhook/test result, and accounting review boundary.
Email: sender/reply-to, test recipient, unsubscribe or notification expectations, and who handles a bounced or misrouted message.
Source data: minimal fields, consent/permission, import sample, retention, export/return, and deletion plan.
Support: first-line tenant operator, Headwaters support path, response expectations under the annual support scope, and escalation conditions.
Rollback: last known accepted configuration, content/data restore or re-import approach, launch pause authority, and communication plan.
Incident and recovery path
Tenant operator records the time, user role, screen/workflow, expected behavior, actual behavior, and whether personal or financial data is involved.
Operator pauses the affected workflow when continuing could duplicate payments, expose data, or corrupt records.
Headwaters classifies the issue as content/configuration, access, platform defect, integration, security/privacy, or legal/accounting.
Headwaters restores the last accepted configuration or disables the affected optional layer when safe. No destructive data change is made to “make it work” without tenant approval.
Escalate security/privacy, legal, accounting, payment, and sponsor-brand issues to the named reviewer.
Record resolution, user communication, evidence preserved, and whether the roadmap or scope must change.
Evidence retained after handoff
Retain the approved map and ledger version, decision register, signed scope, data manifest and permissions, access matrix, configuration/test record, training record, launch acceptance, baseline, results report, sponsor approvals, support/incident log, and roadmap revision. Do not retain raw personal or financial data merely as evidence when an aggregate or redacted record is enough.

8. 30/60/90-day operating rhythm
Point	Review	Minimum evidence	Decision
30 days	Can the named operators complete the core workflow? What is confusing or fragile?	Use notes, support questions, completion/adoption counts where available, and one operator interview	Stabilize, clarify, or pause
60 days	Did the layer reduce the named burden? Is the data/content maintained? Are permissions and support working?	Before/after burden observations, data-quality check, unresolved issues, member/producer feedback where collected	Continue, adjust scope, or prepare next funding step
90 days	Is the layer worth sustaining and is the next tool now justified?	Results report update, cost/support review, roadmap ranking, tenant and sponsor decision	Renew, add a standard tool, commission custom work, or stop
9. Paper-ready handoff sequence
At the end of an engagement, the tenant receives a tenant-facing handoff folder containing:

“What we mapped” — approved organizational map and language decisions.
“What we launched” — selected tool, settings, roles, and known limits.
“How to run it” — role quick references, training completion, weekly/monthly rhythm.
“How to get help” — support owner, incident path, recovery boundary, and renewal terms.
“What we can add later” — roadmap with funding trigger and separate-scope flags.
The handoff is about operating the tenant's configured layer. It is not a handoff of Headwaters platform administration or shared infrastructure.
