Yes. The three-offer structure is coherent, and **continuity** is the shared commercial idea:

1. **Co-ops & Organizations — Governance Continuity**
2. **Family Teams — Care Continuity**
3. **Sovereign Ops — AI Continuity**

They are not three unrelated services. They are three applications of the same promise:

> **Important knowledge, authority, and intent should survive handoffs without being silently rewritten or trapped in one person, institution, or AI platform.**

# Recommended offer architecture

## 1. Co-ops & Organizations

### Public name

**Governance Continuity**

### Buyer

- Co-operatives
- Nonprofits and community organizations
- Homeschool networks
- Rural and northern organizations
- Member-serving organizations
- Boards coordinating work across staff, volunteers, partners, and funders

### Problem

The organization’s real operating knowledge is fragmented across:

- board minutes;
- spreadsheets;
- email and text threads;
- individual staff members;
- informal community vocabulary;
- grant documents;
- partner systems.

When a director, coordinator, volunteer, vendor, or funding program changes, the organization must reconstruct what was meant, authorized, promised, and learned.

### Promise

> **Preserve the decisions, source meaning, operating knowledge, and authority your organization needs to continue when people or systems change.**

### What the engagement produces

- Governance and workflow map
- Role and authority map
- Vocabulary and translation review
- Refused-actions and prohibited-conversions register
- Critical handoff templates
- Decision and correction history
- Export and succession plan
- Founder/key-person continuity plan
- One bounded digital workflow or organizational implementation
- Training and handover

### Strongest existing proof

The 807 Food Co-operative work demonstrates:

- board-governed workflow boundaries;
- source-preserving handoffs;
- distinction between a planning signal and a commitment;
- human review;
- vocabulary governance;
- corrections and contestations;
- privacy-safe baseline measurement;
- continuity without turning the co-op into a merchant or AI-controlled operation.

This must be presented as a **bounded demonstrator and reference deployment**, not proof that a universal organizational continuity platform is complete.

### Pricing foundation

The repository currently supports this public starting structure:

- **Eligible nonprofit, co-operative, or community engagement:** starting around **$20,000**
- **Commercial organization engagement:** starting around **$28,000**
- Expanded pilots, regulated workflows, integrations, migrations, research, and sensitive data: scoped separately

### Pricing conflict to resolve

Current documents contain different ongoing models:

- per-member pricing of approximately $1.25–$2.50/member/month;
- fixed annual licensing that explicitly avoids a per-member levy;
- annual maintenance estimates;
- negotiated white-label licensing.

These should **not all appear publicly at once**. Until the governance model is resolved, the safest public framing is:

> Starting implementation price, with hosting, maintenance, training, and expanded scope quoted separately.

Do not publicly promise a per-member model yet.

## 2. Family Teams

### Public name

**Family Team Plan**

### Continuity category

**Care Continuity**

### Buyer

- Working parents
- Homeschool families
- Larger families with different child needs
- Families balancing land, household, care, and paid-work responsibilities
- Families frustrated by screen and transition conflict
- Families who want person-centred planning without clinicalizing one another

### Problem

Most family systems lead with:

- behaviors;
- deficits;
- generic schedules;
- one-size-fits-all expectations;
- invisible labour carried by one parent.

The mother becomes the household’s only continuity system. She holds everyone’s needs, appointments, interests, responsibilities, transitions, and emotional weather in her head.

### Promise

> **Help every person draft their role, needs, and contribution—then turn the whole family’s input into a daily rhythm that makes the work lighter.**

### Product method

The parent facilitates rather than unilaterally defines everyone:

1. Follow each person
2. Ask questions
3. Draft their page
4. Bring it back for correction
5. Identify shared agreements
6. Combine the plans into a family rhythm
7. Review and revise as capacity changes

### Each person’s plan includes

- What I care about
- What I bring to the team
- What I want to learn
- What restores me
- What overwhelms me
- How I prefer to be invited
- What I can reliably contribute
- What I need help carrying
- How others can tell I need support
- What I want the family to understand

### Household plan includes

- Shared goals
- Meals, sleep, and daily anchors
- Work and appointment constraints
- Homeschool and interest-led threads
- Household and land responsibilities
- Screen and AI agreements
- Support pairings
- Seasonal pressures
- Low-capacity alternatives
- The family’s definition of “enough for today”

### Output

The system produces a **Daily Team Rhythm**, not a rigid schedule:

- shared anchors;
- individual routes;
- household contributions;
- learning opportunities;
- screen transitions;
- parent-capacity protection;
- minimum viable day;
- fuller options for higher-capacity days;
- short review and correction loop.

### Entry product

**The Family Screen Reset** is the correct acquisition wedge.

It should produce the first Family Team Agreement:

- what role screens currently serve;
- child-specific transition observations;
- approved, uncertain, and excluded content;
- screen windows and boundaries;
- transition supports;
- low-capacity exceptions;
- what each family member agrees to practise.

Then it offers the next step:

> Build your complete Family Team Plan.

### Product ladder

1. **Free screen-pattern checklist**
2. **Family Screen Reset** — low-cost printable/guided product
3. **Family Team Plan** — complete multi-person guided experience
4. **Daily Team Rhythm** — recurring planning and review
5. **Governed family AI** — later capability inside the established plan

### Existing foundations

- Dependent profiles and access zones
- Learning Eave approval history
- Media Shelf
- Media Pattern Audit
- Cultural Concern guided experience
- Family-steward guide backend
- Care companion moments
- Family, Homemaker, Homeschool, and Permaculture doctrine
- Stripe-gated Print Suite infrastructure

### Honest product status

Built now:

- several individual components;
- guided screen reflection;
- media review;
- guardian controls;
- family/care continuity infrastructure.

Not built yet:

- Family Team Plan data model;
- multi-person drafting workflow;
- household goals;
- daily synthesis;
- flexible day generation;
- integrated review loop.

Marketing must not imply that the complete Family Team Plan is already available.

## 3. Sovereign Ops

### Public name

**Sovereign Ops**

### Continuity category

**AI Continuity**

### Buyer

Initially:

- co-operatives;
- community organizations;
- care organizations;
- rural operators;
- boards evaluating AI use;
- organizations where one coordinator holds too much informal knowledge.

It should not initially be marketed as generic “AI transformation.”

### Problem

Organizations want the capacity benefits of AI, but ordinary AI adoption can create new continuity failures:

- the AI summary replaces the source;
- a possibility becomes a commitment;
- uncertainty disappears;
- authority becomes ambiguous;
- institutional vocabulary overwrites local meaning;
- one provider becomes the organization’s memory;
- nobody can explain what the model changed;
- the organization cannot exit without losing its operating context.

### Promise

> **Use AI to prepare work without transferring your organization’s authority, meaning, or memory to the model.**

### Governing line

> **AI prepares. People authorize. The source remains visible.**

### Appropriate AI functions

- Retrieve
- Compare
- Classify
- Draft
- Identify missing information
- Prepare reviewable packets
- Flag uncertainty
- Summarize exceptions
- Suggest clarification, refusal, deferral, or return to source

### Prohibited authority

AI must not independently:

- authorize payment;
- promise availability;
- confirm a sale;
- make a board decision;
- alter source records;
- infer sensitive characteristics;
- issue an external commitment;
- convert uncertainty into fact;
- become the organization’s final vocabulary authority.

### Deliverable structure

A Sovereign Ops engagement should produce:

1. AI authority map
2. Approved and prohibited workflows
3. Data and source classification
4. Human-review lanes
5. Handoff-envelope design
6. Correction and contestation process
7. Retention, revocation, export, and exit plan
8. Provider-dependency review
9. One bounded pilot workflow
10. Baseline and evaluation plan

### Product maturity

The doctrine and architecture are strong. Several enabling systems are implemented. The reusable generalized offer is not yet fully productized.

The first public offer should therefore be:

> **A bounded AI-continuity assessment and pilot for one organizational workflow.**

Not:

> “We will install an autonomous sovereign AI operating system.”

### Proof language

Use the 807 workflow as a demonstrator:

> Producer supply signal → aggregate demand signal → collection planning → optional distribution handoff

The AI may prepare and clarify the handoff. It may not turn the producer’s possible supply into inventory, an order, a sale, or a delivery promise.

# Shared framework across all three offers

Each lane uses the same continuity questions:

1. **Origin** — Who supplied the information?
2. **Authority** — Who is allowed to decide?
3. **Purpose** — Why is it being used?
4. **Scope** — What is included?
5. **Recipient** — Who may receive it?
6. **Timing** — Is it current, conditional, or expiring?
7. **Uncertainty** — What is missing or contested?
8. **Review** — Who confirms the next step?
9. **Correction** — How can someone challenge or amend it?
10. **Exit** — How can the person or organization revoke, export, or leave?

The language changes by audience, but the structure remains stable.

| Audience | What must survive | Authority remains with |
|---|---|---|
| Co-op/organization | Decisions, vocabulary, responsibilities, handoffs | Board and designated human roles |
| Family Team | Individual voice, care knowledge, agreements, daily rhythm | Family members and guardians |
| Sovereign Ops | Source, uncertainty, permissions, institutional knowledge | Human principals and governing bodies |

# Critical truth and protection rules

Public material must:

- distinguish live, configurable, pilot, and roadmap capabilities;
- never claim that 807 is a complete universal AI platform;
- never call an aggregate signal an order or commitment;
- never imply clinical diagnosis or healthcare approval;
- never imply that Family Team Plan predicts behavior;
- never imply autonomous AI authority;
- keep exact formulas, thresholds, scoring mechanisms, and counsel material private;
- preserve source wording beside translations;
- provide correction, revocation, export, and exit paths;
- avoid publishing confidential partner information;
- avoid unsupported “first,” “only,” scientific, medical, legal, security, or savings claims.
