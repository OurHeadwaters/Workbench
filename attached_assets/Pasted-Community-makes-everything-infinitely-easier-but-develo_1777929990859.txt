Community makes everything infinitely easier, but developing community is not an easy thing. You've got to show up. 

The most valuable communities are loaded with people who want to get things done. They consistently look for opportunities to lift others up along the way and make an impact on those around them. Technology has both brought us together and torn us apart. It's time we recalibrate what it means to have community and find ourselves collectively through individual empowerment. 

Community. 

The way to design community is just like designing a garden. You need an intentional placement of boundaries and intentional facilitation of interactions. Forcing community is like designing a garden in a swamp; You can do everything else right, but you're always going to be covered in muck.

Voluntary interactions and clear definition of boundaries is what it takes for grassroots community to flourish. 

Especially when talking about women, we have been conditioned and (on the whole) biologically lean towards agreeableness. This often comes with insufficiently communicating our boundaries to those around us. 

Working on any project or job, you sometimes can't choose everyone you interact with on a daily basis. It's especially important to have clearly defined boundaries and regular communication with anyone you find difficult, and to work towards interacting with them in a way which gives you a positive (or at least neutral) result.

Preparedness through strong community networks:

A strong community can significantly improve our preparedness in times of need, but building such a community is no easy task. It requires consistent effort and involvement.

The most resilient communities are filled with proactive individuals who seek opportunities to support and uplift one another, making a positive impact on everyone involved. The use of technology has both united and divided us, so it is essential to redefine the concept of community, focusing on individual empowerment to strengthen the collective whole.

Community building.

Creating a thriving community is similar to cultivating a garden; it requires thoughtful planning, clearly defined boundaries, and deliberate facilitation of interactions. Trying to force a community to evolve is akin to attempting to grow a garden in a swamp; no matter how well you do other tasks, you will always be stuck in undesirable conditions.

Grassroots communities grow by encouraging voluntary interactions and clear boundary definitions.

In particular, for women who have been conditioned and often biologically inclined towards agreeableness, it is vital to communicate boundaries effectively.

In professional settings, we do not always have the luxury of choosing our acquaintances. Therefore, having explicit boundaries and regular communication with challenging individuals is essential to achieving positive (or at least neutral) outcomes.
