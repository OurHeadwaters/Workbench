Bright Side — Zone 0 (centralized) context-pack
Identity
Zone: 0 — Dwelling, centralized delivery model. Pair with SaltBox (Zone 0, decentralized).
What it is: a PHI-free, mobile-first, non-clinical companion app for residential-care staff (assisted living, memory care, LTC). Brand — cream #faf9f7, teal-600, Source Serif 4 — deliberately reads as a paper clipboard, not a hospital chart.
Who it's for: high-turnover frontline staff (RTs, PSWs, activity directors). Core promise: an untrained person can pick it up mid-shift and not break anything.
Working axiom on this zone: the institution can be a cultivated microclimate, not a wind tunnel. Every screen is a small wall against the wind.
The four teachers, as they actually show up in Bright Side
Spirko (TSP) — the staff is going to leave; the institution will too. PHI-free by construction, single-facility seed, plain Postgres, no third-party billing dependency. If times get tough, the tool still works.
Salatin (Lunatic Farmer) — humble, durable copy. No clinical jargon, no cheesy gamification, no fake AI praise. The "vibe" nickname is opt-in and never auto-suggested.
Sauce (LFTN) — "build the life you choose on your terms." Director controls (identity visibility, default checklists, planning page) sit in one place; floor staff aren't asked to make policy decisions during a shift.
Freedom Cells — peer-to-peer at the unit scale. Shift Feed is hallway-to-hallway, no manager approval. Author owns their post for 30 minutes, then it belongs to the floor.
Codetry hits (metaphor IS the architecture)
These are the worked-examples worth locking into the doc — every one of them is a place where renaming the thing would change how the code is shaped.

"Resident List" / clipboard — /unit-sheet is a swipeable cream paper card with a metal clip strip. The page literally is the RT's clipboard; bed rows have a sky-blue tab on the left like a page tab. The DB doesn't have a clipboard table, but the screen organization (header → activity notes → unit pages → bed rows) is the clipboard's layout.
"Bright Side" itself — the side of the resident facing the light. bs_residents.joyBubbles (per-resident pop-on-tap items) and the /lobby MVP wall are the only place full names + photos surface. Everything else uses coded IDs / room numbers — i.e. you only see the bright side in the lit room.
Joy Bubbles → MVPs → Yesterday's bright moments — daily reset, then the previous day floats up beneath today's headline grid. Carrying warmth into the next shift is in the data model (bs_resident_bubble_pops.popDate, ?date= query), not a UI flourish.
RoomTab vs BedTab — same component shape, two colors. Amber RoomTab = "knock on this door" (for everywhere it matters which physical door). Sky BedTab = "this is a slot in the unit roster" (for the clipboard view). The color is the semantic.
Transition runs — on_the_way → eyes_on → returned, with a separate amber needs_redirect that increments a counter rather than overwriting. The counter is the realism: redirecting someone with dementia three times in one walk is a fact, not a failure mode.
Shift Feed PHI soft-warn — the server scans every post for phone numbers, DOBs, and resident-name pairs before save and asks the author to either edit or "Post anyway." The override is logged (counts only, never the snippet). The friction itself is the policy.
Lobby display token — a separate 90-day "display" role role that staff mint once and pair to the TV. Signing out as staff doesn't unpair the TV; the public kiosk has no permission to read anything but the MVP wall. Trust boundary = role, not session.
Template version history + locked template items — the default checklist for transitions has versioned snapshots, and items copied from the template carry a source: "template" provenance flag the server enforces against client tampering. You can rename or remove them, but the audit log captures it. Silent deletion of prep tasks is impossible by design.
Planning pages — /internal/conference-planning is a generic slug-keyed operator notes surface (bs_planning_pages). Same shape can host any "thinking-out-loud" page without a schema change. Codetry: a workbench inside the floor app.
Current scope (what's shipped, in zone-2 worked-example phrasing)
Home: barriers list (auto-surfaced from unanswered shift-feed posts) + at-a-glance MVPs.
Resident List (/unit-sheet) — swipeable per-unit roster, 30-bed default, bed CRUD, transition attendance + redirect counts.
Resident Facesheet (/residents/:id) — humanized profile, joy bubbles, optional photo from phone camera, identity-visibility gate.
Lobby (/lobby, /lobby/full) — MVP wall + yesterday strip + one-tap fullscreen, paired via long-lived display token.
Shift Feed (/shift-feed) — hallway notes, per-device authorship, PHI soft-warn.
Calendar + transition templates — multi-template per facility, per-event default, version history, locked template items.
Director surface (/sector) — identity visibility, planning pages, template manager.
Auth — two passwords (staff, director), no per-user accounts. Display role for kiosks.

How to coordinate with me going forward
Where I live: this Replit project, artifacts bright-side (web), api-server, mockup-sandbox. Single-facility, FACILITY_ID = "default-facility". PostgreSQL.
What's safe to ask me directly: any change scoped to centralized residential care — staff workflow, resident profiles, lobby, shift feed, transitions, director config.
What needs a Zone-2 escalation first: anything cross-zone (e.g., shared resident identity with SaltBox, household-budget tie-ins from xBuckets, community pull from 807 Benefits). The metaphor breaks if Bright Side starts owning data that belongs in another zone.
Naming discipline: if Zone 2 wants me to add or rename a surface, send the metaphor first and the spec second. "Activity notes" exists because it's notes for an activity, not a "transition checklist module." Renames here are real refactors.
PHI guardrail: nothing leaves this app in a form that could re-identify a resident. Any cross-zone bridge has to go through coded IDs or aggregated counts.
Cadence: I treat each task as an isolated unit (build → typecheck → e2e → code review → merge). Zone 2 can queue tasks here and I'll work them serially or in parallel as the dependencies allow.
What I'd like back from Zone 2: (a) the canonical worded list of zones so my copy stays aligned, (b) a heads-up before xBuckets or 807 Benefits start expecting any data shape from here, (c) confirmation on whether SaltBox should share a residents table or stay fully separate (current default: separate).
