What this app is

Bright Side is a tool for care-home staff — PSWs, nurses, HCAs — the people doing the floor work in a long-term care home or group home. It's not a charting system. It's the opposite. Existing care software captures the clinical record. Bright Side captures everything that doesn't go in the chart: the resident who had a good morning, the nurse who made sure three people got their moment today, the thing a staff member needs to know before they walk in the door for their shift.

The framing is a fence metaphor — cognitive decline is real, the institutional walls are real, and things don't go back the way they were. Bright Side is a choice to stand on the bright side of that fence and work the small things that still travel through. That metaphor is load-bearing. It's why the words matter so much in this project.

What's working well

The core loop is complete. A staff member picks up a shared tablet, says who they're helping as, and they have: a list of residents with joy bubbles to pop, a hallway wall of shift notes to read and ack, a shadow list of things that need clearing, and a beat sheet for shift handoff. All of that is live, tested, and solid. The director has a real dashboard — analytics, audit logs, nudge management, staff roster, facility settings. The kiosk-mode hygiene (idle auto-clear, "Helping as" picker, visibility-resume) has been tested to a level that would surprise you, including a race-condition test for eight simultaneous acknowledgments.

Two big things just landed while we were talking: Task #212 filled the demo database with 96 lived-in residents — so the 96-bed pilot home demo is no longer empty. And Task #211 built a private market research brief at /market-brief/ — a mobile-first pitch document with competitive landscape, regulatory framing, pilot economics, and walk-in talking points. That one's private-by-default (blocked from search indexing).

The vocabulary discipline is documented. Four codetry passes sit in docs/codetry/ — the methodology record of every naming decision made since the project started. That's rare for a project this size.

What's rough or unfinished

There are two pre-existing typecheck errors in the transition templates that nobody has touched intentionally — they're tracked but parked. There's also a test that flags the word "MVP" in the credits page as a legacy vocabulary violation, which is a pre-existing issue from before the rename landed correctly. Neither of these is breaking anything in production.

The bigger unfinished things are decisions, not bugs:

Pass #004 (the beat sheet and the gather) is still a draft. The sidebar tab still says "Handoff" instead of "Gather." That rename is written and ready, but it's waiting on operator questions at the bottom of the pass document — questions that only you can answer.
The Resident → Neighbour rename hasn't happened. It's the single highest-impact deferred word in the whole app — it would change the frame entirely for community-living facilities — and it's waiting on whether that word will hold politically in a real LTC facility.
There's no mobile companion app yet. The web app installs as a PWA on a phone, but a native Expo app was always on the horizon.
The family digest exists, but it's the only outbound surface. There's no printable shift report, no EHR export, nothing else that crosses the fence.
What it was trying to become

The care-home operating system for the human side of the work. Not the charts — the relationship layer. The shift handoff that doesn't lose anything. The brag wall that makes a PSW's best day visible to their manager. The one-tap "I've got this" that means one person owns a problem instead of three people stepping around it.

The 96-bed pilot is the first real test of whether any of that matters to an actual facility. The market brief is what you'd hand a director before a meeting. Those two things landing together today is good timing.
