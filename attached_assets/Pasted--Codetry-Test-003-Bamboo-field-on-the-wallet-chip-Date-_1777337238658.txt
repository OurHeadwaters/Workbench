# Codetry Test 003 — Bamboo field on the wallet chip

**Date:** 2026-04-26
**Zone:** 1 — Headwaters / xBuckets (daily kitchen garden)
**App:** xbuckets PWA, Payday tab
**Tester:** Bobbie + Replit Agent
**Surface tested:** mockup canvas, shape `bamboo-field-chip`
**File:** `artifacts/mockup-sandbox/src/components/mockups/payday-rain-test/BambooFieldChip.tsx`

---

## The question (one line)

The meta-doc `lineage-and-hempcrete.md` names XRP as **the bamboo field**.
Does the word "bamboo field" earn a place on the wallet/XRP-swap chip — the
quiet plumbing chip that test 001 demoted below the "Channel the rainfall"
CTA — or does it stay as a meta-doc reference only?

## What was on screen

The canvas surface put three chip wordings in the same slot, beneath the
real graduated CTA from test 001 ("Channel the rainfall · $1,200 ready ·
every drop into a bucket"):

- **Variant 0 (control)** — production wording.
  "412 XRP unconverted   ⇄   Swap to ~$420 RLUSD."
  Mechanical, register-neutral, no metaphor lift.
- **Variant A (full metaphor)** — wallet becomes field, swap becomes cut.
  "Bamboo field standing   ✂   Cut ~$420 into rainfall."
  No XRP word visible. Maximum metaphor commitment.
- **Variant B (half-metaphor)** — bamboo field as the holding's nickname.
  "Bamboo field · 412 XRP   ⇄   Swap to ~$420 RLUSD."
  XRP retained as the anchor noun; bamboo field added as a label.

The test was: read each chip cold, with the rainfall language sitting
right above it, and ask whether the user can tell what the chip does
from the chip alone.

## Falsifiable claim

"Bamboo field" earns the chip if a cold reader, with no prior context,
can tell from the chip alone what the chip does. It fails if the bamboo
word lands as a non-sequitur next to rainfall / channel / bucket — i.e.
if the metaphor word does a second job (importing a biological register
onto a water-register screen) and so does neither cleanly. This is the
test 001 rule — *one word → one referent* — extended one level up:
*one register per screen*. Two metaphors competing for the same surface
is the same defect as two referents competing for the same word.

## What happened, read cold

- **Variant 0** reads as plumbing, exactly as test 001 left it. The
  reader knows the chip swaps XRP into RLUSD. No poetry, no confusion.
  The chip does not pretend to participate in the rainfall metaphor — it
  is the wiring underneath, and that is what the demotion in test 001
  earned.
- **Variant A** breaks. "Bamboo field standing" with no XRP word leaves
  the cold reader with no idea what the chip touches — it could be a
  garden, a savings goal, a metaphor easter-egg. "Cut into rainfall" is
  worse: it asks the reader to hold *biological cutting* and *atmospheric
  rainfall* in the same gesture, on the same screen, in the same
  sentence. The metaphor cracks at the verb. The screen now has two
  unreconciled registers — water (rainfall, channel, bucket) and plant
  (bamboo, cut, standing) — competing for the reader's attention. This
  is precisely the failure mode test 001 named, only one level higher:
  not "one word doing two jobs" but "one screen carrying two metaphors."
- **Variant B** is the most interesting failure. "Bamboo field · 412
  XRP" doesn't crack — it just adds noise. The XRP word still does all
  the load-bearing work; "Bamboo field" sits as a nickname that gives
  the reader nothing they didn't already have. The chip is no clearer
  than Variant 0, and it is heavier. The metaphor is paying no rent.

Bobbie's reaction in plain words: *"Variant A makes me think the app
just changed subject. Variant B is fine, but I don't know why bamboo is
there. Variant 0 is just the chip."*

## The rule discovered

> **One register per screen.** The codetry rule from test 001 (one word
> → one referent) extends to the screen as a whole: a single product
> surface should carry a single metaphor register, not two competing
> ones. A metaphor that lives honestly in the lineage doc may still be
> the wrong word for a surface, if that surface already belongs to a
> different register.

This is not a contradiction of the bamboo-field meta-doc. The meta-doc
was right that XRP-as-renewable-upstream is the honest framing of the
holding *as a thing in the world*. It was right that "asset" / "wallet"
flatten the holding into the portfolio register and lose dignity. The
meta-doc remains the place for that finding.

What this test discovered is that *naming the upstream on the same
screen as the downstream* is the move that breaks. The Payday tab is
the rainfall screen. The chip on it is rainfall plumbing. The bamboo
field stands one room over — referenced from the lineage doc, not
written onto the rainfall surface.

## Verdict

**Rejected for the wallet chip.** The bamboo-field metaphor stays as a
meta-doc reference only. No production change to
`artifacts/xbuckets/src/pages/PaydayPlanner.tsx`; no copy parity change
to `copy.fr.ts`. The chip on the Payday tab continues to read as the
quiet plumbing chip test 001 demoted it to.

The canvas variants are preserved (`BambooFieldChip.tsx`) as a
counter-test artefact: a record of what was tried and why it didn't
graduate.

## What would change the verdict

The metaphor would earn the chip — or some other surface — if either
of these became true:

1. **A bamboo-only surface is built.** If the upstream gets its own
   screen (e.g. a "your bamboo field" view that does not also carry
   rainfall language), the metaphor would have a clean register to
   live in and could be tested there on its own terms. Test 001's
   demotion of the wallet chip is what makes this possible; the
   upstream is no longer fighting for the Payday tab's CTA slot.
2. **The water register itself is replaced.** A radical rewrite of
   the Payday tab into the bamboo register (cut → cure → store →
   share) could, in principle, host the field word natively. This
   would be a much bigger move than a chip rewrite, and is not on
   the table from this test.
