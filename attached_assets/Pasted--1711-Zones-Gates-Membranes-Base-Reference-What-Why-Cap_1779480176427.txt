The Model (base layer)
Three Zone Types
Protected by the Eave (0, 1, 2 in the constitutional framing):

Zone	Name	Character
Z1	Circle	Private household identity — the innermost protected layer
Z2	Workbench	Operational / practitioner layer — liminal, has crossings on both sides
Z3	Community	Above-board organizational identity (XRPL wallet, public-facing)
Gates as Membranes
A gate is not a wall. It is a protective, functional material at the overlap between zones — the place where zones visually and operationally touch. Gates:

Are the interactive edges — where action happens between zones
Are permeable by design, not locked by default
Look different at every crossing (the materials list varies by context)
Provide resilience, not isolation
The base formula is the hempcrete analogy: a known structural material that breathes, insulates, and flexes. The "extended materials list" is what gets added per context (thermal mass, vapor barriers, acoustic layers, etc.) — each gate gets the mix it needs.

The Two Constitutional Gates (from the Eave Rule)
Z1–Z2 Gate Household context may cross in controlled form — enough to know whose work this is — but private identity does not expose outward toward Z3.

Z2–Z3 Gate Contractor identification may appear in controlled, auditable form at the crossing (e.g. for contracts or audit events), but must not persist as a stored reference inside Z2-scoped records. The audit/regulatory layer (the "giraffe") may have sight across this gate, but the audit query shape must not be composable into a Z3→Z1 reverse lookup.

Z1–Z3 Absolute Prohibition No direct or composable path may ever connect a Zone 3 wallet address or derived identifier to any Zone 1 household record. This is not a gate — it is an architectural absence.

What a Gate Looks Like in Practice
At the personal–working edge: a consent step, a role switch, a session context change
At the working–community edge: a credential presented at the crossing but not stored inside
At a group-to-group edge: a shared protocol (a meeting format, a data standard, a mutual agreement) that both sides recognize
The gate is always visible — you know you are crossing it
The Membrane Principle
Zones overlap. The membrane is not at the hard boundary — it is in the overlap region itself. Two zones sharing space is normal and healthy. The gate governs what flows through, not whether zones can touch.
