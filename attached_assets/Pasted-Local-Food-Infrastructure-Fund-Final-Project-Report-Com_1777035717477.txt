Local Food Infrastructure Fund - Final Project Report

Community Food Hub Project: Hydroponics, Local Production, and Community Engagement

Project Period: January 1, 2025 – March 2026

Location: Dryden / Wabigoon Area, Ontario

Prepared: March 2026

Executive Summary
The Community Food Hub Project successfully established a sustainable food hub integrating hydroponics, seasonal vegetable production, community workshops, and partnerships to enhance local food access, education, and security in the Dryden area.
Key achievements include:
Hiring and training coordinators (Ryan and Alicia).
Installation and use of hydroponics equipment, cold storage, and supporting infrastructure.
Weekly community engagement (Tues-Thurs for planting/harvesting/processing).
Delivery of diverse workshops (e.g., sourdough, pizza, canning, salsa/meat preservation).
Engagement of approximately 30 families through the Community Vegetable Program.
Strong partnership with Dryden Native Friendship Centre for community meals featuring local produce.
Ongoing seasonal production, gifting/selling of greens, and adaptive events (e.g., weekly at Cloverbelt Farmers Market).
Over 100lbs of local produce donated to the Dryden Food Bank throughout the growing season for their programs and participants.
The project met most timeline milestones despite challenges like low formal attendance at some events and gradual build-up of participation. It fostered community skills in food production, preservation, and nutrition, while strengthening local partnerships. Final participation estimates suggest 100+ unique individuals/families engaged across activities directly, compounded by the donated produce to the Dryden Food Bank. 
The project concludes positively, with recommendations for scaling in future phases (e.g., trailer upgrades completed spring 2026, continued partnerships).
1. Project Overview and Objectives
Goal: To build a community food hub that promotes local hydroponic and seasonal food production, provides education/workshops, hosts community meals/events, and improves food access/security through engagement and partnerships.
Key Objectives (aligned to timeline in planning document):
Assemble team, procure/install equipment, and prepare site.
Deliver training, workshops, and production cycles.
Launch and sustain community events/meals.
Evaluate outcomes and plan for sustainability.
Target Community: Residents of Dryden/Wabigoon area, including families, volunteers, and partners like Dryden Native Friendship Centre.
2. Project Timeline and Achievements
The project followed the planned timeline with strong implementation across phases.
January 2025: Project Kickoff
Team assembled; coordinators (Ryan and Alicia) hired via People and Talent program and trained by Bobbie on kitchen/growing equipment.
Hub lunch program launched to build traffic and showcase local ingredients in a variety of “daily special” offerings.
Community outreach conducted; hydroponics equipment installed and staff/volunteer training completed.
Achievement: Strong foundational setup; immediate community entry point via lunch program.
February – March 2025: Equipment Procurement
Hydroponic production workshop facilitated.
Kitchen equipment, cold storage, tables, chairs, tents ordered/installed (delivery ~8 weeks).
Coordinators planted crops; invited community Thursdays for planting/harvesting; greens gifted/sold.
Cold room installed and used for local ingredients; trailer minimally upgraded (final upgrades spring 2026).
Achievement: Production initiated early; community involvement from outset.
March – April 2025: Site Preparation
Food hub prepared/renovated; parking secured (Wabigoon and Dryden hub locations).
Outdoor spaces cleared for events.
Weekly events at Cloverbelt Farmers Market with Cold Trailer, tables and volunteer-led engagement.
Achievement: Site fully operational; market presence built visibility.
April 2025: Equipment Installation
All equipment (cold storage, tables, tents) installed.
Achievement: Complete and on schedule.
May – August 2025: Training and Workshops
Ongoing training on equipment/food safety.
Workshops delivered: Sourdough, Pizza, Salt Blend, Family Canning (started January onward).
Achievement: High engagement in hands-on learning.
June 2025: Food Production Cycle
Workshops on seasonal cooking, preparation, preservation (monthly).
Engaged ~30 families via Community Vegetable Program (bags of vegetables, usage tips, prep/delivery support).
Achievement: Direct impact on family food access and skills.
July 2025: Community Meal Events Launch
Partnered with Dryden Native Friendship Centre: Featured local produce (e.g., potatoes, corn) in traditional meals.
Partnership strengthened; aligned production with future events.
Photographs documented activities.
Achievement: Culturally relevant integration; enhanced community ties.
May 2025 – March 2026: Continuous Production and Engagement
Weekly Thursday sessions (12-2pm): Prep/processing seasonal vegetables.
Donation of excess produce weekly to Dryden Food Bank in excess of 100lbs through the season.
Achievement: Sustained momentum through season.
January 2026: Evaluation and Adjustments
Reviewed feedback/production: Low formal attendance but high drop-ins.
Participants enjoyed tips for lesser-used vegetables (rutabaga, turnip, zucchini, pumpkin).
Community assisted in weekly preservation activities.
Achievement: Adaptive learning; positive qualitative feedback.
February 2026: Final Preparations
Final reports/documentation prepared.
Community harvest dinner event planned/held to celebrate in September 2026.
Achievement: Culminating celebration planned.
March 2026: Project Completion
Final reports submitted.
Debrief meeting held with team to discuss successes/lessons.
Achievement: Project closed successfully.
3. Key Outcomes and Impacts
Participation: ~30 families in vegetable program; weekly drop-ins; broader reach via market/events/partnerships.
Skills Built: Food production, preservation (canning, freeze-drying), cooking with local/seasonal items.
Food Access: Greens/vegetables gifted/sold; integration into community meals; cold storage for local ingredients.
Partnerships: Strengthened with Dryden Native Friendship Centre; aligned production to cultural/traditional needs.
Sustainability: Hydroponics/cold storage operational; weekly routines established; trailer upgrades pending spring 2026. This trailer will create a food hub on wheels! The production equipment will be utilized in the trailer, available for rent to the community during harvest times! 
4. Challenges and Gaps
Low formal attendance at structured events (high drop-ins instead) → suggests preference for informal/flexible engagement.
Limited quantitative data (e.g., total kg produced, exact participants, cost savings, health impacts) → tracking improved mid-project but not comprehensive.
Trailer upgrades delayed to spring 2026 → minimal impact on 2025 season.
No major budget overruns or safety issues reported; however, formal financial summary unavailable here.
5. Lessons Learned and Recommendations
Informal, drop-in models work well in this community → prioritize flexible Thursday sessions.
Partnerships (e.g., Native Friendship Centre, Dryden Food Bank) amplify impact→ expand to more groups (schools, elders). Issues are often having the organizations to be a week ahead with their ordering cycle and aligning our internal operations.
Hands-on workshops highly valued → continue/expand (e.g., more preservation for winter).
Improve tracking: Use simple logs for production, attendance, feedback.
Future: Complete final infrastructure upgrades / site preparations; seek funding for expanded hydroponics/capacity for ongoing operations without a physical operating lease.
6. Conclusion
The project successfully transformed the Food Hub into a vibrant community resource for local food production, education, and sharing. It built skills, strengthened partnerships, and improved access to fresh, seasonal foods. While some metrics are qualitative, the engagement and positive feedback indicate meaningful progress toward food security. The team thanks all participants, volunteers, coordinators (Ryan, Alicia), and partners for their contributions. Sustainability is strong, with routines in place for ongoing impact.
Sustainability of Program: Without ongoing operations capital or an improved market, the program lease cannot be renewed to further maintain the cold room capacity and indoor hydroponics area past 2026. 
The cold room was donated to food security programs in Deer Lake remote community, allowing them to have infrastructure for a community kitchen. The indoor hydroponics equipment is being incorporated into the harvest trailer offerings alongside the food processing equipment. 
The program will continue for years to come with the use of the LFIF funded trailer, putting the food production equipment hub on wheels for community use. This aligns with our vision of decentralized infrastructure in growth plans for 807 Food Co-operative.
