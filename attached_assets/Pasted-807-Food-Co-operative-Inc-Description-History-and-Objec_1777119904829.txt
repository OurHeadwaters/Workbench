807 Food Co-operative Inc: Description, History and Objectives 
Description & History: 
807 Food Co-op is governed by a board of directors (producers) and aims to build an inclusive community through local food initiatives. Since 2021, through membership collaborations and volunteer efforts, we have created multiple opportunities for social wellness, promoted environmental resilience and fostered an inclusive local economy. We have developed and implemented the following community projects, showing our commitment to enhancing the community alongside improving the local food system. 
We currently have 2 full-time coordinators funded through NOHFC People and Talent, set to expire September 2025, and a number of membership volunteers supporting the activities in the Food Hub Pilot Program. 
Mission: 
To empower communities in Northwestern Ontario by utilizing food as a catalyst for change. Through our grassroots food hub, we strive to foster self-sufficiency, sustainability, and social impact. By connecting local producers, consumers, and resources, we build a resilient food system that supports the well-being and prosperity of our communities." 
Vision: 
Our vision is to design a system where communities rely on vibrant hubs that aspire to empower, inform and educate generations to come, enabling them to embrace their inherent potential and actively participate in building resilient, self-reliant communities. 
Goals & Objectives: 
● Promote local food production, reducing dependency on centralized food systems and fostering biodiversity 
● Encourage the development and adoption of innovative agroecological practices, improving soil health and minimizing environmental impact 
● Support local businesses and farmers, creating inclusive job opportunities and bringing economic development to communities 
● Build strong networks for knowledge sharing and skill development, enhancing community resilience and cooperation 
● Foster positive change, affecting not only the way people grow, consume, and distribute food but also their connection to the environment and each other

Summary of Project: Developing an Economically Sustainable Community Hub Model 
807 Food Co-operative is working to expand our pilot program into a fully realized, economically sustainable food hub model. Operating in the rural region of Northwestern Ontario, we face unique challenges—chiefly, the need to increase member and community engagement. We believe that fostering public education and strengthening local food producer relationships are vital to building a reliable revenue stream, ensuring long-term sustainability. 
We are seeking to partner with experienced professionals to enhance our efforts in securing the necessary financial resources and strategic guidance to create a resilient, impactful food hub. This partnership will not only secure our fiscal health but also empower us to build a cooperative model capable of withstanding audits, ensuring future growth. 
Additionally, by bringing on experts in community and business development, we will create a business strategy that actively engages our members, promotes inclusion, and guarantees the financial sustainability of our social impact initiatives. 
Given the challenges of the Northwestern Ontario market, particularly in securing skilled labor, we aim to partner with a creative development and marketing contractor to help elevate our marketing and outreach efforts. This collaboration will drive awareness, increase community involvement, and create growth opportunities for both the co-operative and our member businesses. 
How This Work Will Advance Progress in the Following Sectors: 
Social Wellness: 
● Community Connection: Hosting workshops and events for the public, led by co-op members, to build skills and connections within the community. Recent workshops include canning, food preservation, sourdough baking, and hydroponic gardening. 
● Youth Engagement: Through partnerships with local schools, we offer opportunities for students, especially those with disabilities, to engage in work experience, boosting their community participation and well-being. 
Environmental Resilience: 
● Waste Reduction: Our Food Hub Pilot has introduced several sustainability programs, including a jar recycling initiative, egg carton re-utilization program, and scrap harnessing for homesteaders. 
● Sustainable Agriculture Education: We are committed to educating both the public and our membership on the benefits of regenerative farming practices, which promote long-term environmental health and local economic sustainability. 
2
Inclusive Economy: 
● Supporting Women Entrepreneurs: We provide a pathway for women to launch or expand their businesses through access to our licensed Food Hub, offering resources, networking, and production opportunities. 
● Inclusive Employment: Our partnership with the local high school Co-op program supports youth with disabilities, helping them gain valuable work experience and improve their quality of life by reducing barriers to meaningful employment in the community. 
How the grant will be used: 
Developing an Economically Sustainable Community Hub Model is a $40,000 project to support our co-operative’s strategic business development cycle through two targeted streams of professional services: 
● Business Development and Financial Support ($20,000): We will engage with a community development contractor to engage an accountant and bookkeeping  professional for financial oversight. This contractor will work with the financial support team to better develop operational systems for our co-operative and offer business planning and strategies to generate revenue. This will establish a strong financial foundation and an actionable roadmap as pilot funding ends. 
● Marketing and Promotion ($20,000): We will hire a creative development and marketing contractor to design materials and execute a robust marketing strategy. This will amplify our visibility and revenue potential, addressing volunteer burnout by building a sustainable income stream. 
Impacts on the development of our co-operative enterprise:
This dual investment will accelerate our transition to a self-sustaining co-operative by strengthening financial stability and market presence. The outcomes we aim to achieve include: 
● Financial Resilience and Strategic Clarity: Professional accounting and business planning will ensure accurate financial management and a clear growth strategy, reducing risks and enabling informed decisions. 
● Revenue Growth: Effective marketing will boost local food industry awareness and community engagement, generating income to support qualified labour and reduce reliance on volunteer efforts. 
● Operational Sustainability: Together, these efforts will alleviate founder burnout by shifting focus from crisis management to proactive development, fostering a thriving co-operative. 
● Enhanced Credibility: A solid financial backbone and professional marketing will position us as a credible, competitive enterprise, attracting partners and members. 
Everything is in place to immediately engage in this project as we aim to align it with funding from the Accelerator Fund to cover the budget of this project in 2025-2026.

Partnership and Supports 

Access to Technical, Strategic, and Administrative Support 
The Developing an Economically Sustainable Community Hub Model project benefits from robust technical, strategic, and administrative support critical to its success. The Community Development Contractor has access to the team at Ontario Co-operative Association (OCA), who provides technical assistance through project management tools and governance training, equipping us to streamline operations and build resilient hubs. 
Support from the Co-op Sector 
Our initiative is strongly backed by the co-op sector, as we were initially funded through Local Food and Farm Co-op, and later being managed by OCA, a key association serving 93 members across Ontario, including Northern Ontario. With a $550,000 budget and a team of 5, OCA delivers clear examples of support: access to governance workshops, financial oversight resources, and advocacy to elevate our profile. This aligns with their mission to foster sustainable co-operative communities, directly strengthening our project’s framework and long-term viability. 
Other Partners and Their Contributions 
● DHS Student Co-op Program: Supporting Co-op Placements for Students with Disabilities to enhance community inclusion 
● Rooted Impact: This women-led social enterprise drives marketing, crafting strategies to boost visibility and revenue, reducing volunteer burnout and sustaining economic growth. 
● Patricia Area Community Endeavours (PACE): As a Community Futures Development Corporation, PACE provides business counseling and resources, enhancing operational capacity and infrastructure. 
● Local Line: Their platform offers logistical tools, streamlining distribution and connecting producers to consumers, improving market access. 
● Superior Seasons: Led by Kevin Belluz, they expand markets in Thunder Bay and Dryden, modeling scalable hub practices. 
● Rock Front Family Farm: Tyler and Jackie Bernier’s microgreens farm in Sioux Lookout contributes a replicable sustainability model, bolstering local economic impact. 
Together, these partners ensure technical proficiency, strategic direction, and administrative stability, driving our co-operative’s success in Northwestern Ontario.
