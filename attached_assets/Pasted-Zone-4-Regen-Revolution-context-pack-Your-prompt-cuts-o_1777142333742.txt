Worked examples (citable in the codetry working-doc)
Each of these is a place where naming changed structure, not decoration:

"Per-customer share split" replacing "cut sheet management" as the v0.1 centerpiece. Pre-Karen strawman optimized a symptom. The real work was the math after the kill — 6–14 customers, one animal, by hand, twice a week. Renaming the centerpiece rewrote the schema, the success metrics, and the build sequence in one move.
"Don't fix my book." Karen's line. We dropped the booking calendar from v0.1 entirely. Naming her paper ledger as a working artifact (not a legacy obstacle) removed a feature.
"Failures land on the tool, not on her." Black Barn's tone note. Now an architectural constraint on every error state — operator-protective copy, not generic "something went wrong."
"Make it easier for Karen to say no, not just easier to say yes." Lisa Koshel's add. A v0.1 design principle, not a feature: scope-creep guards, lock-date enforcement, no-button-that-just-absorbs-pain.
OMAFRA traceability as byproduct, not feature. The animal record was designed so the regulator's log falls out for free. Naming it "byproduct" prevented a parallel ledger from being built.
Estimate → actual → variance loop. Karen records real packaged weights; the same UI that showed "≈" to the farmer flips to "Actual" when the data lands; the dashboard surfaces per-species variance; the constants in yield.ts stay tunable from that signal. Naming the whole cycle as a loop, not three features, kept it coherent.
