The repo answers the pricing question clearly: $120/business already exists as Producer Premium; it is not a hypothetical new middle tier. The current structure is:

Producer Basic — $50/year: one vote, public producer profile, producer badge, and direct discovery by local households.
Producer Premium — $120/year: everything in Basic, plus Snap to Shelf photo inventory, sales/payout dashboard, featured marketing rotation, 0% producer-to-producer commission, a Google Doc export of the producer’s own course, and priority event sign-up.
Coordinator — $300/year: the board’s forecast model already treats this as the higher-value network-organizer role.
Community / Sustaining / Harvest — $5/$10/$20 monthly: these are household/community support memberships, not business tiers.
My recommendation is keep $50 and $120, but rename and communicate the $120 tier as “Producer Pro” or “Network Producer,” not as a vague middle tier. At $120/year ($10/month equivalent), it is a credible “operating toolkit” price: the producer gets tools that help them be found, publish inventory, understand payouts, promote themselves, and trade with peers without 807 becoming the merchant or intermediary.

The value proposition should be:

Join for $50 to have a voice and be discoverable. Choose $120 to run your producer presence with the shared tools.

Communicate it in three layers:

Outcome first: “Be easier to find, keep your listings current, and turn local connections into repeat business.”
Tool proof second: show the real tools already in the repo — public profile/discovery, Snap to Shelf, payout visibility, producer-to-producer at-cost purchasing, featured promotion, course export, and event priority.
Boundary third: “You keep your customer relationships and transactions. 807 provides the shared discovery, tooling, and cooperative frame; it does not promise to broker every sale.”
Do not lead with “support the co-op,” because that explains the $50 but not the $120 upgrade. Also avoid promising guaranteed sales, homepage placement on demand, or unlimited administration. Featured marketing should be framed as an eligibility/rotation benefit, and 0% peer commission should be described with its existing scope.

For the 5→100 expansion, the strongest message is not “pay more for a listing.” It is:

The $50 tier gives every business a seat in the network. The $120 tier gives active producers the tools to participate in it.

That lets you recruit beyond farmers: food processors, prepared-food businesses, makers, tradespeople, logistics helpers, and other local businesses can join when they benefit from discovery and connection. Coordinators remain the $300 organizer role, rather than forcing every business into a high-touch package.

One caution: the current public copy says the Premium price is “provisional” and may change by board vote. Keep that governance note until the board formally approves the commercial model.
