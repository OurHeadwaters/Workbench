# Codetry Test 001 — Payday: words bearing weight

**Date:** 2026-04-26
**Zone:** 1 — Headwaters / xBuckets (daily kitchen garden)
**App:** xbuckets PWA, Payday tab
**Tester:** Bobbie + Replit Agent
**Surface tested:** mockup canvas, shape `rain-test-a`
**File:** `artifacts/mockup-sandbox/src/components/mockups/payday-rain-test/RainIsIncome.tsx`

---

## The finding (one line)

When three CTAs share one metaphor, the words stop bearing weight. The user
reads the screen and freezes — the rain language no longer points to anything
specific because it points at three things at once.

## What was on screen (before)

The Payday tab surfaced three actions, side by side, all dressed in water
language with no priority signal:

1. **"Let's make it rain"** — the hero CTA that executed the pour.
2. **"Convert XRP → RLUSD"** — funded the reservoir (wallet swap).
3. **"Full rain / 90% / 75% / 50% / Custom"** — a percentage chip that
   scaled how much of the plan to pour.

Three actions, one metaphor, no hierarchy. Bobbie's reaction in plain words:
*"I didn't know what action to take."* The rain word was being asked to do
three jobs and so did none of them.

## The intervention (Variant A on canvas)

Collapse the rain word back to a single referent. Give every other step in
the flow its own word, and let each word carry exactly one job.

- **"Today's rainfall"** card replaces the percentage chips. The figure
  is the actual combined income for *this* payday — not a percent of plan.
  Stream rows (Paycheck $1,500, Side gig $340) sum to the rainfall total
  ($1,840) and are editable via a "Wobble" toggle. Rainfall is fluid by
  nature; the input acknowledges that.
- **Flow ribbon** makes the sequence visible: Rainfall → Siphon →
  Reservoir, with dollar values ($1,840 → −$640 → $1,200). Each node has
  one icon, one number, one label.
- **"Channel the rainfall"** is now the only CTA. Subhead: *"$1,200 ready ·
  every drop into a bucket."*
- **Wallet / XRP swap** demoted to a quiet plumbing chip below the CTA —
  it is plumbing, not a competing call.

## The rule discovered

> **One word → one referent.** In codetry, every metaphor word in the
> surface UI must point to exactly one thing in the architecture. The
> moment a word names two actions, it names neither.

Word map after the test (no overlaps):

| Word       | Refers to                                                      |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rainfall   | Income arriving this payday (paycheck + side gig combined)     |
| Siphon     | Bills auto-paid out of the rainfall before the reservoir       |
| Reservoir  | The stablecoin wallet — what's left after the siphon           |
| Channel    | The act of distributing reservoir → buckets ("every drop")     |
| Bucket     | A named envelope (Groceries, Rent share, Transit, …)           |

Each word, one job. None overlap. The metaphor IS the architecture, end to
end, with no slack between word and referent.

## Falsifiable claim

If we hold one-word-one-referent across the constellation, ambiguity at
decision points should drop. Conversely: when a user hesitates at a
screen, the diagnostic move is to look first for a word doing two jobs.

This test is falsified if Bobbie or another user, looking at Variant A
without prior context, still cannot tell from the words alone what the
single next action is.

## Later sightings

Two short notes added after graduation. Both are downstream sightings of the
same rule on the same screen, but they tell quite different stories about
how locked vocabulary survives — or doesn't — in normal day-to-day work.

### Sighting 1 — the catch in the wild (2026-04-26)

After the graduation, Bobbie pulled up a fresh screenshot of the live Payday
tab and read the catchment-plan card line by line. The middle row read
**"Stays in bank (bills)"**. The number was right; the word wasn't. *Bank*
is a banking-vocabulary word. The locked Zone 1 word for "money leaving the
rainfall before it reaches the reservoir" is **Siphon** (Test 002). The
banking word had quietly slipped onto a water-vocabulary surface — exactly
the kind of micro-defect Test 001's rule is meant to catch.

The fix was a one-line rename in two parallel surfaces — `PaydayTab.tsx`
and `PaydayPlanner.tsx` — both reading **"Siphoned out for bills"** instead.
End to end the maintenance took less than a minute. The rule did its job:
the diagnostic move ("when a word feels off, look for one doing two jobs
or one belonging to a different register") found the defect immediately,
and the locked word map (Test 002) provided the exact replacement word with
no design discussion needed.

The point of the sighting is that the rule lives in upkeep, not just at
variant-time. Most of codetry's day-to-day work isn't running new tests on
the canvas — it's catching small drift in production copy and routing it
back to the locked map.

### Sighting 2 — the rebase that silently un-did the fix (2026-04-26)

The more interesting half. Two days after Sighting 1, while drafting Test
001a's appendix, a search of the codebase showed the **planner** surface
(`PaydayPlanner.tsx` line 1223) still reading **"Stays in bank (bills)"**.
The wording fix had only stuck on the tab. The planner had been silently
restored to the older copy.

What happened: Task #806 (the calm rest view) was branched from a
pre-rebase tree that pre-dated Sighting 1. When that branch landed, it
rebased onto the post-graduation main — and the rebase produced a tangled
conflict in the catchment-plan card, which was resolved by pulling the
planner card forward from the pre-rebase branch. That branch had never
seen the Sighting-1 fix, so the older banking word came back along with
the rest of the card. Nothing in the merge process flagged it. The
locked word map exists in `docs/codetry/002-zone-1-words-locked.md`,
not in any check the merge tool ran.

The lesson the sighting forces:

> **Locked vocabulary drifts on merges unless something machine-checked
> holds it.** The map being written down in a doc is necessary but not
> sufficient. The word map needs at least one mechanical witness — a
> forbidden-strings unit test, an ESLint rule, a copy-snapshot diff, or
> all three — that fails the build when a banned word appears on a
> surface it doesn't own.

This sighting is what graduates the rule from "discipline a person holds"
to "contract the toolchain enforces." A discipline degrades on every
rebase, every long branch, every fast merge. A contract doesn't.

The fix here was a one-line edit (planner regression undone, parity with
the tab restored) plus a single new lock-in unit test in
`PaydayPlannerRestView.test.tsx` that asserts the catchment card reads
"Siphoned out for bills" and not anything containing "Stays in bank". One
test isn't a guard for the whole locked map — but it's the smallest
possible mechanical witness, and now that surface is held.

### Follow-up

A general machine-enforced guard for the locked Zone 1 word map is a
candidate next test (forbidden-strings unit test, lint rule, or
copy-snapshot diff). Proposed as a separate follow-up; not implemented
in 001a.
