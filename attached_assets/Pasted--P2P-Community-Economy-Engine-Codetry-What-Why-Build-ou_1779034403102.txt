 - P2P Community Economy Engine — Codetry

What & Why

Build out the peer-to-peer community economy engine inside the Codetry platform. This is a time-banking and mutual aid system designed specifically for northern Indigenous reserve communities, where informal value exchange is already happening constantly — the platform's job is to make that value legible, trackable, and spendable. This is grounded in northern economic principles and real (material) reconciliation, not performative gestures.

The founder describes the engine as "already built — it just needs oil and four tires." The core mechanics exist; this task is about tightening the onboarding, trust, and adoption layers so the system can actually reach critical mass in a community context.

The Zone Model (Adoption Strategy)

The community is organized into adoption zones. Design and language should reflect this:

Zone 4/5 — Early Adopters: Self-organized, motivated by direct lifestyle improvement. Will adopt without incentives because the tool genuinely improves their daily life. These users pull the next ring in organically.
Zone 2/3 — Incentive-Driven: Need visible perks and rewards to engage. The system must deliver a clear payoff before asking for effort. Referral mechanics and reward loops are the lever here.
Zone 1 — Full Community: The eventual target. Reached once critical mass exists and the network itself is the incentive.
The reserve geography is a structural advantage: dense, walkable, high social trust. Network effects hit faster than in dispersed populations. Build with this in mind — don't over-engineer for scale before you've cracked local depth.

The Smallest Valuable Action (Core Mechanic)

The on-ramp that converts hesitant users into active participants is:

A defined amount of personal time converted into actual credit or cash, spendable on real things.
This is the moment the system becomes real. Everything before it is setup; everything after it is habit. The design must make this moment:

Legible — Plain language. "You just earned $X credit, spendable at Y." No ambiguity, no jargon, no "pending."
Immediate — Credit lands before the user closes the app. Delay kills belief.
Spendable today — There must be something worth spending credit on right now, not once the network grows.
Shareable — The first user must have a natural reason to pull in the second. (e.g., "Refer someone and you both get a bonus hour credited.")
Tip Function

The founder has confirmed the tip function (tipping for information/knowledge shared) needs to be always-on. This is the primary P2P crypto transaction vector and should be treated as a first-class feature, not an add-on.

Wallet / Sign-On Architecture

Sign-on friction is a known risk. The agreed approach:

Progressive wallet reveal: Users sign up with just an email. A wallet is created silently in the background. The user only "meets" their wallet at the moment of their first real transaction (sending or receiving a tip or credit). Motivation is highest at that moment — use it.
Hybrid custodial/non-custodial from day one: The data model must support both paths. Custodial (platform manages keys) lowers friction for Zone 2/3 users. Non-custodial (user holds keys) is available for Zone 4/5 types who want full sovereignty. Do NOT build custodial-only and bolt on self-custody later — that migration is painful. Plan for both in the schema now.
Get them to the finish line: Setup motivation is high when there's a real transaction waiting. Design the onboarding arc so the first meaningful action (earning or spending) is the reward for completing setup — not a promise of future reward.
Three Layers That Must Be Solid

1. Trust Layer

Credits must feel permanent and real. No ambiguity about whether they'll disappear, expire without warning, or get devalued.
Transparent transaction history the user owns.
Clear terms: what a credit is worth, what backs it, and who controls it.
2. Governance Layer

Who sets conversion rates (time → credit → dollar equivalent)?
Who resolves disputes?
How does the community own this system rather than renting it from an outside platform?
These don't all need to be solved in v1, but the UI should leave space for community governance to plug in — don't hard-code founder decisions as permanent.
3. Growth Layer

The zone model needs mechanical support: different entry points, reward tiers, and referral flows for different zone types.
Zone 2/3 users need a perk or reward visible before they complete setup — give them a reason to push through the wallet friction.
Zone 4/5 users need depth and autonomy — don't over-simplify for them.
Done Looks Like

A user can sign up with just an email and immediately see what the platform offers.
A user can record a time contribution (helping hands) and see a credit land in plain language before leaving the app.
A user can send a tip for information to another community member via the P2P tip function.
A user who completes setup encounters their wallet naturally, at the moment of first real transaction — not as a gate.
The hybrid custodial/non-custodial wallet paths are supported in the data model even if only custodial is exposed in v1 UI.
The zone-based referral hook exists: completing setup gives the user a shareable link or action that benefits both them and the person they bring in.
Out of Scope

Full governance tooling (rate-setting UI, dispute resolution workflows) — leave architectural space but don't build it yet.
Non-reserve or large-scale deployment optimization — build for the dense walkable community first.
Fiat on/off ramp integrations — credit-to-spend within the network is v1; cash-out is later.
Context & Voice

This platform is grounded in northern economic principles and genuine reconciliation — the kind that materially changes the daily conditions of people's lives, not the kind that produces land acknowledgments. The language and design should reflect that: plain, direct, community-owned in feel, not startup-polished. Value flows that already exist in the community are being made visible and amplified — the platform is infrastructure, not the hero.
