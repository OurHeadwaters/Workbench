## I. The Standby — Full Role Definition

*The chapter narration introduces The Standby in prose built for a general reader. What follows is the complete role definition from the deleted working document — the full vocabulary, the severity ladder, the two sub-shelves rejected as umbrella names, and the codetry principle the naming process produced.*

---

The constellation's first non-zone primitive. The umbrella system + always-on state for *temporary centralized disruptions* — drought, fire, smoke, flood, ice, power, water, freight, payment systems, pandemic, evacuation, AGM-postponed, key-person-down. Six-word vocabulary: *the Standby* (umbrella + always-on state), *a call* (a specific active event), *the watch* (active-monitoring posture), *standby stock* (the always-on reserves), *the debrief* (after-action synthesis), *centralized disruption* (the class of event). Four-rung severity ladder: advisory / standby / active / standdown. Two sub-shelves inside the umbrella: *The Common Pantry* (food/supply sub-noun) and *The Watch* (active-monitoring sub-noun) — each rejected as the umbrella name in turn because neither could hold both states.

The takeaway is the new codetry principle that came with it: **the name has to hold both states** — when a system has both a slow side (always-on practice) and a fast side (active event), the name has to do both jobs in one word, or the system will fork into two systems with two cultures.

## II. The Gate — Full Role Definition

*The chapter narration introduces The Gate in the same register as The Standby. What follows is the complete role definition — the full vocabulary, the four-rung severity ladder, the four single-direction names rejected on paper, and the second codetry principle the naming process produced.*

---

A calm membrane between *the bright side* (the constellation's own dialect: neighbour, channel, the books, standby stock, the watch) and *massity* (mass-society dialect: resident, bank account, financial statements, inventory reserves, compliance officer). Holds the umbrella system + always-on posture for passing language across institutional boundaries — codetry vocabulary going out to a regulator, banker, funder, lawyer, or other legacy-world counterparty; their vocabulary coming back in.

Eight-word vocabulary: *the Gate* (umbrella + always-on posture), *the bright side* (codetry-vocabulary side), *massity* (legacy-world side), *a mapping* (a registered correspondence between a bright-side term and its massity equivalent), *a substitution* (one applied instance of a mapping), *a category* (the domain a mapping belongs to), *a translation* (the auditable record of what crossed and what changed), *a calm membrane* (the metaphor doing the load — selectively permeable, alive, not a wall and not an open door). Four-rung severity ladder: draft / under-review / cleared / refused — the *refused* rung is first-class, for source-side language with no honest target-side equivalent the founder is unwilling to lose.

Four single-side or single-direction names rejected on paper: *Translator* (collapses posture into pure transaction), *Filter* (subtractive mis-frame), *Censor* (carries punitive weight the metaphor refuses), *Glossary* (dictionary without the room).

The takeaway is the second new codetry principle: **the name has to hold both sides** — when a system has language that has to live in two contexts that hold different vocabularies as legitimate, the umbrella name has to fit both contexts in one word, or the system will pick a dialect and lose the other room.

## III. Both-States and Both-Sides — The Structural Comparison

*Chapter 4 treats both-states and both-sides in separate sections, in the order they were discovered. This passage puts the two principles side by side — the structural comparison that shows where they agree, where they diverge, and what the divergence means for naming a primitive.*

---

Both tests are pick-one-then-the-other, but the axis is different. The both-states test picks a *state* … The both-sides test picks a *context* … In both tests, if the answer to either question is *not really*, the system has already started forking — into two cultures (both-states) or into two pipes (both-sides). If the answer to both is *yes — same word, different rung* (both-states) or *yes — same word, different room* (both-sides), the name is holding and the system is one system.

`artifacts/codetry-handbook/data/handbook.ts, lines 428–429`

---

The axis distinction is the load-bearing difference. Both-states is a temporal test: the system runs at two speeds, and the umbrella name has to survive the tempo change. Both-sides is a contextual test: the system faces two rooms simultaneously, and the umbrella name has to survive the room change. A system can fail one test without failing the other. The Standby fails only the both-states test if you force it — it holds in both states but would not survive being read as a both-sides primitive, because *standby* is not a term of art in massity-dialect. The Gate fails only the both-sides test if you force it — it holds from bright side and from massity, but it does not run at two tempos; it runs at one tempo in two simultaneous contexts.

The phrase that holds both: *same word, different rung* (for states); *same word, different room* (for sides). If you can say that sentence about your umbrella name, the name is doing its job.

## IV. The Refused — A Bar-Level Worked Example

*Chapter 4 introduces the Refused outcome as a Gate rung and names it as first-class. The passage held here is the bar-level structural analysis — what Refused looks like when read from each side of the Gate, and why the finding is the same finding in both directions.*

---

*Refused* — the sub-shelf for source-side language that has no honest equivalent on the other side — is the sharpest bar-level worked example. From the bright side: Refused records that a word in the constellation's dialect cannot be translated without distorting it, and so will not be. From the massity side: Refused records that a word in mass-society dialect has been examined and found to have no honest landing point in the constellation's vocabulary. The finding is the same finding read from two directions. Both rooms are served by knowing the Gate will not invent false equivalents.

`artifacts/codetry-handbook/data/handbook.ts, lines 421–423`

---

The Parr's Jars smoked salt crossing (passage 4-R in the harvest) is the field demonstration. The health unit's form had no slot for *freeze-dried microgreens from our own hydroponics, blended with onions from Walls Farm* — only for *kale powder (commercial source)*. The Gate Refused: no honest massity equivalent existed. The product crossed; the story did not. The lesson the field example adds to the bar-level definition: the Gate does not always find an equivalent. Sometimes it refuses. When it refuses, the practitioner notes what was lost in the crossing and keeps the bright-side language alive at home, even when it cannot appear on the label.

Refused is not a failure state and it is not a footnote. It is the discipline proving that the Gate is a gate and not an open door. A system without a Refused rung is a translation machine — it will always find an equivalent, which means it will always invent one when none exists. The Refused rung is what makes the Gate honest.

## V. Bright Side — Data Sovereignty at the Facility Level

*Chapter 2 introduces Bright Side as a mobile-first staff tool for residential care — what it replaces, what it does not ask staff to change, and how the builder describes the tool in his own words. The passage held here is the data-sovereignty framing: what single-facility control means, and what it means for a community that has watched institutional tools take their data with them on the way out.*

---

It starts as a single-facility tool. No head office, no corporate subscription, no vendor lock-in. Your home controls the data. If you stop using it, you leave with a clean export.

`artifacts/codetry-ship/src/pages/BrightSidePage.tsx, lines 117–119`

---

This sentence is doing more structural work than it appears to. In residential care, the data that lives in a facility's care management system is not neutral — it is the institutional memory of what the shift looked like, who was present, what was noted, what was missed. When that data lives in a corporate platform, the facility's memory lives in a vendor relationship. If the relationship ends, the memory is at risk.

Single-facility design is the codetry move applied to data sovereignty: the home controls the data the same way the community controls the noun. Both are anti-drift positions. The vendor cannot take the word with them on the way out; the vendor cannot take the records with them on the way out. The clean-export guarantee is the data equivalent of the codetry-test — a check that the sovereignty claim is real and not just marketing.

For communities building care infrastructure, this framing matters beyond the residential care application. Any tool that collects operational data is making a quiet claim about who owns that data. Single-facility, no head office, clean export is the full claim. The chapter version introduces the tool; this passage names the principle underneath it.
