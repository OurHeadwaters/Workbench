1–2–3 Capacity-Building Action Plan & 45-Day Launch Program

Ryan, you offer a valuable service that solves direct, immediate problems for older adults and less tech-focused individuals in Northwestern Ontario. You provide the patient, in-home guidance that telecom retailers cannot. To grow this while working a full-time job, the business must move from informal, one-off assistance to a structured, highly credible service that you can operate independently. This is the blueprint.

Current Service Reality

You currently offer a robust suite of tech interventions:

(A) Tech Support & Guidance

From email account creation to basic Q&A, familiarizing users with their technology.

(B) Set-Up & Data Transfers

Old-to-new device setups, cross-OS migrations, and peripheral configuration.

(C) Cleaning & Optimization

Malicious ad removal, OS updating, and efficiency tools.

(D) Purchasing & Selling

Sourcing safe, reliable stock to act as transfer/demo pieces and retail inventory.

(E) CAPS (Clerical & Admin Support)

Leveraging writing skills to assist seniors with pension documentation and form submissions.

The Problem & Recommended Shift

The Problem: A flat $60/hour rate untethered from a defined outcome feels open-ended and expensive to new customers. Operating without a visible, professional digital footprint makes people question if the business is a scam. Furthermore, relying entirely on one-off hourly calls makes it impossible to efficiently schedule around your full-time job.

The Shift: Service-first positioning. Lead with fixed-outcome packages while retaining hourly billing only where a package is not practical. A professional, founder-visible presence and formal sales materials for community groups will reduce skepticism and make the service easier to understand, book, and refer.

Risks & Safeguards

Operating a tech support business in a small region carries specific risks. We build these safeguards into your operational plan from day one:

1. The Bell Conflict

Risk: Perception of conflict of interest with your employer.
Safeguard: Strict adherence to positioning Gizmos and Gadgets as an in-home mobile support and clinic service, entirely distinct from telecom retail sales.

2. Inventory Holding

Risk: Tying up vital cash flow in depreciating tech hardware.
Safeguard: Treating devices strictly as transfer/demo pieces rather than adopting a primary retail holding model. Avoid taking on debt to buy inventory.

3. Privacy & Data

Risk: Mishandling sensitive information belonging to vulnerable elderly clients.
Safeguard: Establishing clear, written data-handling protocols and waivers that protect both the client and your business.

4. CAPS (Clerical Support) Boundaries

Risk: Accidentally assuming legal or financial liability when helping with pensions.
Safeguard: Clear boundary documentation proving you act strictly as an administrative assistant, not an authorized representative.

5. Used Devices

Risk: Acquiring stolen, carrier-blocked, locked, or poor-condition hardware.
Safeguard: Use documented IMEI/blacklist, activation-lock, battery, condition, data-erasure, return, and warranty checks before accepting or selling a device.
